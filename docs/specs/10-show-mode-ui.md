## 1. Purpose and scope

The operator app is the window a volunteer runs a Sunday from. It is a client of the engine: it shows what the engine reports, sends commands and edits, and holds no show state of its own (ARCHITECTURE.md). This spec settles how the app and the engine find each other and stay in step, what the app reads from the engine, every part of the show-mode window, the keys, the edits show mode makes, the settings window, the operator lock, and what a fresh install writes. It refines the UX spec's sections on the main window, the top bar, the keyboard, settings and first-run defaults, and carries over what the clickable demo settled; where they disagree, this spec wins. Nothing in `app/` is on `main` yet. The engine side of previews, multiviewer tiles, thumbnails and metrics is, since Area 5, and there is no API server: the engine is driven over OSC.

**In scope:** the app and the engine as two processes, how the app starts, finds and restarts the engine, and the local socket and Mach channel between them (C3, R2); the API's listeners, and the topics, queries and requests the app uses (S2); the main window and each of its regions, carrying the deferred parts of Specs 6–9 (the Playlists and Library windows, the grid, the Media pool tab, the Popups tab's timers, the restore pickers, the badges and the notes the app sends); the browsed-versus-live colour (S10); the keys (R1, R6); the edits show mode makes (S7); view settings (S8); the window at laptop size (S9); the settings window (S11, S12) and the operator lock (S13); first run, with which screens become outputs (S3); accessibility and localization; and the corrections this makes to Specs 1–9 and to the repository's documents (§17).

**Out of scope:** edit mode, the text editor and the small editors (Spec 11, Areas 10 and 14), apart from how show mode opens them; the history view and runtime undo (v1.5); the Archive tab, detach, promote, relink and where-used (v1.5, with library mode); popup templates (v1.5, S6); live inputs, the pool's Sources group and freeze (v1.5); MIDI and Stream Deck devices and the scheduler column (v1.5); roles beyond the lock, and the role badge (v1.5); the sync targets in the UX spec's Network and Startup and backup rows (v1.5's distribution conventions); favourites in the pool and optimize-on-import (v1.5); the multiviewer as an output kind, NDI output and key and fill (v1.5); the pre-flight report (v1.5); slides in the pool (v2, the slide designer); the Timeline tab (v2); a light appearance and languages other than English (later).

**Rules every screen obeys**

- The app sends commands, edits and the requests of §3, and nothing else. Every button, menu item, key and drag maps to one of them (ADR 0002). The requests control the engine process, the lock and the operator's own screen, not the show, which is why they sit outside the vocabulary (§3).
- What the app shows comes from the engine's events and queries (§3). It keeps no state of its own apart from its view settings (§4) and the operator's selection.
- The app writes nothing in the roots but `machine/view.json` and the engine's console log, which it opens for the engine (§2), and it never touches the Keychain (Spec 5 §1, R2).
- An action the app can't take now, because the engine is away, the lock is on or the library is read-only, shows disabled with the reason. Nothing is queued to send later, apart from the note that the engine ended (§3).
- The app never waits on the engine on its main thread. A cue fires on the press of the mouse button; everything else acts on release, as macOS controls do.
- AppKit draws the grid, the strip, the trees and the editors; SwiftUI the settings window and simple panels (S1, written into `CLAUDE.md`, §17).

## 2. The app and the engine

**Two processes** (C3). The app is `Project O.app`; the engine is `oengine`, inside it at `Contents/Helpers/oengine`, built from the same commit. Each outlives the other's crash: if the app dies, the outputs keep running, and if the engine dies, the app shows it and starts it again (Spec 4 §12).

**Where the roots are.** The app keeps the paths of the library root and the machine root in its own preferences, set at first run (§13). Everything else lives in the roots.

**Starting.** The app connects to the engine's local socket, `engine.sock` in the machine root. If an engine answers, the app uses it, whoever started it. Otherwise the app starts `oengine run --library <root> --machine <root> --operator-screen display:<uuid>` in a session of its own, so the engine outlives the app. `--operator-screen` names the screen holding the app's window, and at first run the app adds `--campus "NAME"`; the engine reads both only when it writes first-run documents (§13). When the app starts an engine after the last one ended, it adds `--relaunch` (below), and `--released display:<uuid>` for each screen it has released while it runs (§9). The app opens `machine/logs/engine-console.log` for appending as the engine's standard output and error, so a failure before the engine's own logger starts can still be read, and the engine trims the file in place to its last 10 MB at launch. The app waits up to 15 seconds for the socket, showing "Starting the engine…". An engine that exits with status 3 found another engine holding the root (below), so the app keeps waiting for the socket and doesn't count it as an end; any other exit before the socket comes up is an end (below). If no socket has come up after those 15 seconds, the app shows the console log's last lines and offers Restart the engine (below).

**When the engine is ready.** It writes any first-run documents before Spec 9 §4's first step, and opens its local socket and its LAN listener at that list's step 5, when it takes commands, so `hello` means it is ready.

**One engine per machine root.** The engine holds an exclusive lock on `machine/.engine.lock` while it runs, with its process id in the file. A second engine on the same root exits at once with status 3 and a line saying which engine holds it. A socket file left by a crash is removed by the next engine before it listens.

**The engine as a process.** It runs as an accessory process, with no Dock icon and no menu bar. Its output windows sit at the screen-saver window level as non-activating panels on every Space, which stay put through Mission Control and Show Desktop: they never take the keyboard or make the engine the active app, and they absorb the clicks that land on them, so a click on an output never reaches a window beneath it. It holds a latency-critical activity so macOS never naps or throttles it, and while any output presents it keeps the displays and the system from sleeping. A display output whose screen isn't there stays `absent` and opens when a screen with its id appears; it never falls back to another screen. When macOS asks it to quit (logout, restart, shutdown) or sends it a terminate signal, it stops on request as Spec 9 §4 says.

**The local socket** (R2). A Unix domain socket that only its owner can open (mode 0600), and the engine closes any connection whose peer, by `getpeereid`, is another user. It carries Spec 2 §9's messages, one JSON object per line. No token is sent: the engine's first message is `hello`, naming its version, the API version, its process id, the machine, the run and the Mach service (below). The app names each command's source, `ui`, `key` or `commandbar` (Spec 2 §5), and the engine takes it from this socket only; a LAN client's commands are always `api`.

```json
{ "type": "hello", "api": 1, "engine": "0.4.0", "pid": 4182, "machine": "7a7a…", "run": "51e2…",
  "mach": "org.lcbc.projecto.engine.51e2…" }
{ "id": 41, "type": "command", "source": "key", "command": { "cmd": "playlist.next" } }
```

The socket's path must fit macOS's limit of 103 bytes, so first run refuses a location that would make it longer (§13).

**The LAN API** is Spec 2 §9's WebSocket on `apiPort`, with the token and Bonjour. At launch the engine reads its two Keychain items, the token and the passcode (§12), on a background thread, and makes the token if there is none, so a Keychain prompt after an update comes when the app first starts the new build, while someone is there, and never holds back the outputs (R2). Until it has the token, the LAN listener stays closed and the `engine` topic says so (§3). The app's Info.plist describes its local network use (`NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`, and `NSBonjourServices` with `_projecto._tcp`). macOS asks on behalf of the app that started the engine, may refuse Bonjour and OSC sends until someone answers, and may ask again after an update (ADR 0007), so the engine retries a refused registration or send every few seconds, and the `engine` topic shows the LAN as waiting meanwhile.

**The Mach channel** carries what is too frequent or too large for the socket: the preview surfaces, the channel pictures and the meters (Spec 4 §10). The engine registers a Mach service in the user's bootstrap namespace under the name `hello` gives, with `bootstrap_check_in`, or, where launchd refuses a name it hasn't seen, the deprecated `bootstrap_register`, behind a seam of its own (Spec 4 §14); the app looks it up. Neither needs a launchd file or an entitlement (ADR 0007). Area 19 proves the call on the dev Mac at its first stop for review; if neither works there, a per-user launchd agent that declares the name takes their place, and this section is corrected. Over the channel the engine sends:

- **Previews**: each stack's preview as three IOSurfaces, sent as Mach ports with the stack's number and size, at connect and whenever a stack's surfaces are made again. After each preview frame completes, a message names the stack and the surface that holds the newest frame, at most 30 a second per stack, and the engine writes the next frame into the oldest of the three, so the app reads only finished frames.
- **Channel pictures** (choice U-3): a live picture of each channel for the strip (§7), 256 by 144 pixels, as three IOSurfaces per channel, sent with the channel's number at connect, when a channel is added or renumbered, and whenever they are made again, as after a GPU reset (Spec 4 §12); when a channel is removed, a message names it and the app drops its surfaces. After each picture frame a message names the channel and the surface that holds the newest, and the engine writes them in turn as it does the previews. An aux pass, at most 30 times a second like the previews, makes them for each channel with pixel content: its channel texture, graded and mid-transition as the channel pass leaves it (Spec 4 §7), fit into the picture over black and filtered in one pass, without a mip chain, so it doesn't alias. A channel whose media is missing gets a black picture, never its texture's old pixels. While the app holds the Mach channel, the pictures are a consumer at the preview rate (Spec 4 §2): on each preview tick, a channel with pixel content that no stack pass has sampled since the last one runs its channel pass as aux work, at its render size, or at 1920 by 1080 when that is `largest` and no stack holds it. Text gets no picture, since it has no look until a box gives it one: the cell shows the text itself (§7).
- **Meters**: 15 times a second, for each channel whose content has sound, the peak and RMS level in dBFS since the last frame, after gain, of its loudest audio channel.

If the Mach channel fails, the preview tiles and the strip's pictures say so, and everything else works.

**When an output covers the app.** While the engine is connected and reports an output `presenting` on the screen holding the main window, whether on purpose on a one-screen Mac, by an edit from elsewhere, or at a launch, and the window isn't raised above the outputs (§9), the main window ignores mouse presses, drags and scrolls, and the keys keep working. Raised, or with the engine away, it takes clicks. For ten seconds after an output starts covering it, a note above the output says "Project O is behind output 2. Press ⌃⌥⌘⎋ to take it off this screen." (§9), except for an output kept with §11's Keep, which the app remembers in `view.json`.

**When the engine stops.** From its first catalog on, the app keeps a black, borderless curtain, on every Space, on the screen of each display output other than the main window's, one level below the outputs, so that when the engine's windows go, those screens show black and not the desktop (Spec 4 §12). A curtain sits hidden behind its output while the engine runs, takes any click that lands on it so the desktop never gets one, and closes when the app quits. If the socket closes and the process has gone, a banner across the window says "The engine stopped. Restarting…", and the app starts it again. The relaunched engine puts the last state back (below). While it's away, the library stays browsable as the app last read it, every control that sends something is disabled, and a key that would send a command is dropped, never queued. The app starts it again every time it ends (choice U-7): at once for the first three ends within two minutes, then five seconds after each, not counting restarts the operator asked for. From the third such end the banner says how many there have been and shows the console log's last lines, so whoever is there can see why; Spec 9 §4's guard keeps a last state from coming back when the engine ended within a minute of putting it back, so a state that ends the engine at once comes back once at most.

**What a relaunch brings back** (choice U-14). A launch is a relaunch when the app says so with `--relaunch`, having seen the engine end or ended it itself, or when the run before it ended without a stop in this boot of the Mac: `state/last.json` says `clean: false` and was written after the Mac started. The flag covers a full disk, which removes that file (Spec 9 §7). A relaunch puts the last state back under Spec 9 §4's guards (less than two hours old, and not one just put back), whatever `restoreLastState` says, and never runs the startup macro. So an engine crash, or Restart the engine below, costs a moment of black and not the service. `restoreLastState` then decides only for a launch after the Mac itself went down.

**When it doesn't answer.** The app sends `ping` every two seconds. With no answer within five seconds, the banner says "The engine isn't responding" and offers "Restart the engine", which says the screens go black for a moment and, while the `saving` topic counts unsaved documents, that they will be lost. It ends the engine by its `pid` with a kill, never a terminate signal, so the shutdown macro doesn't run and the run doesn't count as clean, and starts it again, as a relaunch. The app never ends the engine on its own, since the outputs may still be playing. An app that gets no `hello`, from an engine holding the root or within 15 seconds of starting one, offers the same restart, finding the engine by the process id in `.engine.lock`, and starts one when nothing holds the root.

**Versions.** An app and an engine of different versions work together while `api` matches; About shows both. When `api` differs, the app can't drive the engine and asks to restart it, the same way. When the app finds an engine of another build running, as after Keep outputs running and an update, it offers to restart it while nothing is live, so the new build's Keychain prompt comes while someone is there (R2).

**Quitting** (choice U-2). ⌘Q, or closing the main window, asks "Quit Project O? The outputs will stop.", adding "3 changes aren't saved yet" while the `saving` topic counts any, with Quit, Keep outputs running and Cancel. Quit commits any open field (§10), sends `stop`, waits up to ten seconds for its answer, shows the documents the answer names as unsaved (Spec 9 §7), then quits; with no answer in that time, it kills the engine by its `pid`, since Quit asked for the outputs to stop. Keep outputs running quits the app alone, and the next launch of the app finds the engine and connects. A logout, a restart or a shutdown doesn't ask, and the engine stops on the system's request.

## 3. What the app reads and sends

**Connecting.** The app subscribes to every topic below that it uses, then asks for `state`, `catalog` and `media` (queries, below). A connection carries answers and events in the order the engine produces them, so an answer reflects every event sent before it: the app applies the events that arrive after an answer and drops those that came before it. An event carries the `seq` of the log line that caused it, or of the last line logged before it. After each `library` event (Spec 5 §2), the app asks again for the documents the event names that it holds.

**Topics.** Spec 2 §9 names four, and Specs 5, 8 and 9 add four more. The app uses these:

| Topic | Defined in | Carries | Sent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `live` | Spec 7 §6 | The pointer and the armed trigger, and here also `playlistTitle`, `listTitle` and the cue's `label`, from the kept copies when they are gone; and on a fire that enters another list, `boundary`, whose `reset` is false when that list tracks through (Spec 3 §6) | At each fire, load, arm and disarm, and after an edit or reload that changes them |
| `channels` | Here | One event per channel: its content (kind, ref, label, missing, whether it has a picture and whether it has sound, and for timed media its duration, position, loop and end behaviour; for text its fields), state as `/o/channel/N/state` reports it, provenance, lock, tracked attributes with their origins (Spec 3 §2), and, once each, a transition that starts (picture and sound durations and the sound's offset), `steppedOn` and `blocked` | On every change, and once a second while timed media plays |
| `outputs` | Here | Per output: its live stack, its state (`presenting`, `paused` while its screen or card is gone, `released`, or `absent` when its device isn't there) and, for SDI, its reference (`locked`, `freeRun` or `none`). Per stack: blackout and muted channels | On change |
| `audio` | Here | Per audio output: `ok`, `fallback` or `silent` (Spec 4 §12), and muted | On change |
| `popups` | Here | Per slot: its text, and the milliseconds left on a pending clear-after | On change |
| `timers` | Spec 6 §8 | | |
| `library` | Spec 5 §2 | Also cause `lock` (§12) | |
| `media` | Spec 8 §7 | | |
| `history` | Spec 9 §5 | Each group's `source` too (§10) | |
| `saving` | Spec 9 §7 | | |
| `thumbnails` | Here | A cue thumbnail written, with its stack, list, cue and file; or a pool tile written, with its id and file | When written |
| `engine` | Here | The run; the lock and whether a passcode is set (§12); the LAN listener's state (`open`, `closed` or `waiting`) and client count; the last state this run, put back with what didn't come back, or skipped with Spec 9 §2's reason; and badges. Also remote activity | On subscribe and on change; remote activity at most ten times a second |

```json
{ "type": "event", "topic": "channels", "channel": 1, "state": "playing", "lock": false,
  "content": { "kind": "media", "ref": "sha256:9f2c…", "label": "Ocean loop", "missing": false, "hasPicture": true, "hasAudio": true,
               "durationMs": 30000, "positionMs": 1200, "loop": true, "endBehavior": "loop" },
  "provenance": { "kind": "cue", "list": "a9f0…", "cue": "11aa…" },
  "attributes": { "grade": { "value": 3, "origin": "tracked" }, "gain": { "value": 0, "origin": "default" } },
  "transition": { "ms": 1000, "audioMs": 500, "audioOffsetMs": 0 }, "seq": 4822 }
{ "type": "event", "topic": "outputs", "output": 1, "stack": 1, "state": "presenting", "reference": null }
{ "type": "event", "topic": "outputs", "stack": 2, "blackout": false, "muted": [3] }
{ "type": "event", "topic": "engine", "run": "51e2…", "lock": false, "passcode": true,
  "lan": { "state": "open", "clients": 2 }, "lastState": { "restored": false, "reason": "old" },
  "badges": [ { "kind": "thermal", "level": "serious" } ] }
{ "type": "event", "topic": "engine", "remote": ["osc"] }
```

An empty channel has `content: null`. Timed media's duration and position count within its in and out points, so its remaining time is the duration less the position, which the app counts on its own clock between the once-a-second events. A badge's `kind` is `thermal` (serious or critical), `memory` (warning or critical pressure), `watchdog` or `gpu` (a recovery or a device reset within the last five minutes), `clock` (running on a fallback clock, Spec 4 §2) or `keychain` (an item can't be read, §12); the disk is the `saving` topic's and audio devices the `audio` topic's. `remote` lists the sources of the commands that arrived from outside the app since the last such event: `osc`, `api`, `midi`, `streamdeck`.

LAN clients also have `preview`, which the app doesn't use: a JPEG of each stack the client names when it subscribes (`"stacks": [1, 2]`), 480 pixels wide, twice a second (Spec 4 §10). The `commands` topic stays as Spec 2 §9 has it.

**Queries** (Spec 2 §9's `query`):

| `what` | Returns |
| --- | --- |
| `state` | ShowState (Spec 3 §2), the timers, and the `live`, `channels`, `popups`, `outputs` and `audio` events as their topics would send them now |

| `listUse` | For a list (by `listUuid` or number), the playlists that link it, archived ones marked: `{"playlists": [{"uuid": "2b2b…", "title": "Sunday", "archived": false}]}` |
| `versions`, `snapshots` | Spec 9 §6 |
| `thumbnails` | For a list (by `listUuid`, number or `local` uuid) and `stack` S, each cue's thumbnail file or null. The engine queues the missing ones, and a `thumbnails` event follows each |

**Results.** Spec 2 §9's result answers every command. An applied edit's result also carries its `group` and, as `applied`, each of its commands as the log keeps it, with what the engine picked, and its inverse, so the app can find a new object, or revert exactly what it sent without reaching for `undo`, whose top step may be another source's:

```json
{ "id": 52, "type": "result", "result": "ok", "seq": 5120, "group": "g-1c2d3e4f",
  "applied": [ { "command": { "cmd": "edit.create", "object": "list", "list": 57, … },
                 "inverse": { "cmd": "edit.delete", "object": "list", "list": 57 } } ] }
{ "id": 53, "type": "result", "result": "rejected", "reason": "badPasscode: the passcode is wrong" }
```

**Requests.** Beside Spec 2 §9's `command`, `commands`, `subscribe`, `query` and `auth` and Spec 9's `note`, the local socket takes these. On the LAN they are rejected with `localOnly`. They aren't commands: they control the engine process, the lock and the operator's own screen, they carry secrets or concern this Mac's screens, and only the app can send them, the one exception to ADR 0002's rule (ARCHITECTURE.md, corrected in §17).

| `type` | Does | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ping` | Nothing | `pong` |
| `stop` | Stops the engine on request (Spec 9 §4) | `ok` with `unsaved`, the documents it couldn't write (Spec 9 §7), before it exits |
| `releaseScreen` | Takes every output off the named screen for the rest of the run (§9) | `ok` with the outputs released |
| `passcode`, `unlock`, `resetPasscode`, `token`, `newToken` | §12 | |

**Notes.** The app notes these UI-only moments in the command log (Spec 9 §2), and nothing else:

| `event` | When | Fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `appStarted` | The app connects to an engine | `app`, its version; `launched`, whether it started the engine |
| `browse` | The grid shows another playlist or list | `playlist` (uuid) or `list` (number) |
| `backToLive` | Back to live | |
| `editMode` | Edit mode opens or closes | `on` |
| `engineEnded` | The app saw the engine end; sent to the next engine it connects to, the one thing the app holds back (§1) | `status`, when the app started that engine; `restart`, whether Restart the engine ended it; `count`, how many ends the note stands for, since the app last connected to an engine |
| `outputConfirm` | The confirmation of an output on the app's screen ends (§11) | `output`; `kept` |

**Thumbnails and tiles are files.** The engine writes cue thumbnails, pool tiles and edit mode's pictures in `machine/thumbnails/` (Spec 4 §10, Spec 8 §5, Spec 11 §3), and the app reads them there, by the file each answer or event names, relative to the machine root.

## 4. The main window

**Regions**, as the UX spec and the demo lay them out:

| Region | Contents | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top bar | Status, and a few buttons (§8) | 38 points high |
| Left column | Playlists window over Library window (§5) | 248 points wide by default, 200 to 360 |
| Center | Cue grid over the Media pool panel (§6) | The rest |
| Right column | Preview, next row, tabbed panel, channel strip (§7) | 380 points wide by default, 340 to 480 |

The window is at least 1440 by 900 points, or the screen's visible area where that is smaller, as on a 1440-by-900 laptop under its menu bar (S9), and it can go full screen. While it is less than 1000 points high the tabbed panel collapses to its tab bar, at launch and on resize, whatever `view.json` says, and the strip takes the space; above that, `view.json`'s state applies.

**One main window.** Closing it is ⌘Q (§2). It can't be hidden or minimized: the app has no Hide, Minimize or Zoom items and no Window menu, so ⌘H and ⌘M do nothing. The app opens it on a screen no output uses, and moves it to one when it ends up on an output's screen, moved by hand or by a change of screens, if such a screen exists. Settings opens on the main window's screen and moves the same way, and every confirmation and file dialog is a sheet on the main window, apart from §11's confirmation over an output, which sits above the outputs.

**Appearance.** Dark only in v1, with the demo's palette as named colours. Apart from the chips' own legend (§6), four colours carry meaning everywhere: red for live and for errors, green for outputting and for the selected preview, amber for browsing away from live (§6) and for warnings, and blue for selection and focus. Colour is never the only signal (§15).

**Focus.** The left column is a browser: its search fields take typing without touching the show keys (UX spec). The grid, the pool, the right column and the top bar never take typing; text fields do, and the keys follow §9's rules.

**View settings** (S8) are in `machine/view.json`: the window's frame, the column widths, the left column's split, the bottom panel's state and height, whether the tabbed panel is collapsed, the preview's stack and the multiviewer, the grid's elements and density, the pool's folder, sort, kind filter and tile size, the strip's view, whether archived playlists show, the open folders in the Library tree, the last thing browsed, recently used media, the run whose restore banner was dismissed (§14), and edit mode's last page and each page's selection (Spec 11 §2). The defaults are the UX spec's first-run layout: the divider at default, the panel open, the grid showing thumbnails, chips, labels and triggers with notes off, the multiviewer off, the preview on the thumbnail stack, and the strip showing all channels. The app writes the file a second after the last change, atomically (Spec 1 §5). The machine root is never synced, so it stays with the machine. ## 6. The center

**Browsing and live** (S10). The live context is the live playlist, or the list fired from the library when that is live. While the grid shows anything else, it is browsing: the top bar, the grid's edge and Back to Live turn amber, and the grid's header says "Browsing". A song opened from the Library while its playlist is live is browsing too. Scrolling within the live playlist isn't browsing. A fire from the grid makes what it fired the live context, so the grid stops browsing; a fire from anywhere else (a key, a trigger, OSC) leaves the grid where it is. Back to Live (⌘L, or the button) returns the grid to the live context and scrolls to the live cue; ⌘L also scrolls to the live cue when it is out of view without browsing. With nothing live there is no amber.

**Following the live cue.** The grid scrolls only when the live cue is out of view, and by the least amount. It never scrolls while the pointer has moved over the grid in the last second, while a mouse button is down or while a drag is under way; it catches up when the pointer leaves the grid or rests, so a press never lands on a cue the grid moved under it.

**The grid header** carries the title (a playlist's with its date, or a list's), the number of lists or of cues, the search field (⌘F; it highlights the cues whose label or text matches and scrolls to the first; Escape clears it), and the View menu: chips, trigger glyph, notes, and Compact, which drops the thumbnails. It shows "updated" after its master reloads from disk (Spec 1 §8) until the list is next fired, and "missing" in red, with no cues, when its list is gone. ## 9. Keys

**The binding table is the feature** (UX spec): each binding maps a key on a device to one command, stored in `keys.json` (Spec 1 §3). In v1 the device is the keyboard; MIDI and Stream Deck devices and the scheduler's rows are v1.5. The app reads the table and sends a binding's command with source `key` when its key is pressed. Bindings are absolute, never "the selected channel" (Spec 2 §5), and fire on a key's first press only: the repeats of a held key never fire it again, so holding Space sends one `next`.

**Key names.** A binding's `key` is a key name, after any modifiers, joined by `+` in the order `ctrl`, `alt`, `shift`: `space`, `right`, `shift+escape`, `ctrl+alt+f`. The names are `a` to `z`, `0` to `9`, `f1` to `f20`, `space`, `return`, `escape`, `tab`, `delete`, `forwarddelete`, `left`, `right`, `up`, `down`, `home`, `end`, `pageup`, `pagedown`, the keypad's `pad0` to `pad9`, `padenter`, `padplus`, `padminus`, `padstar`, `padslash`, `paddot` and `padequals`, and the punctuation keys by their unshifted US character (`-`, `=`, `[`, `]`, `\`, `;`, `'`, `,`, `.`, `/`, and the grave accent). A key is matched by its position, named as on a US keyboard, so a binding stays on its key whatever the input source. Some keys are reserved and can't be bound: ⌘-combinations belong to the menus; Tab, ⇧Tab, ⌥ with the arrows, ⌥Return, ⌥Space and ⌃Return are the keyboard's navigation (§15); plain `escape` leaves fields and closes things; and ⌃⌥⌘⎋ is fixed (below). The engine refuses a binding of a reserved key, or a key name outside this grammar, with `badValue` (Spec 5 §6), so an import can't bring one in.

**Defaults** (R1): Space and → send `next`, ← sends `previous`, and ⇧⎋ sends `system clearall`. No per-channel clear keys ship, and section jumps ship unbound (UX spec). The demo's B, "blackout the previewed stack", isn't a binding, since a binding can't name the selection; a campus binds `stack 1 blackout toggle` instead.

**When bindings fire.** Whenever a Project O window is the key window, a key goes to the first of these that is open or has the focus, and to the bindings only when none of them takes it:

1. **The key recorder** (§11) takes every key: it records the next press, for up to ten seconds ("Press a key…"), and says what that key already does ("⇧⎋ is Clear all"). Plain Escape cancels it; ⌃⌥⌘⎋, a system hot key, still works.
2. **An open menu** takes every key until it closes, ⇧⎋ included, as macOS has it; the keys overlay says so.
3. **A file dialog**, or a list that has the focus in a sheet or popover, takes every key but ⇧⎋.
4. **A text field** takes every key but ⇧⎋, a binding that includes `ctrl`, and the function keys `f1` to `f20` (the UX spec's focus rule, made exact). So a `shift+` or `alt+` binding on a character key doesn't fire while someone types, which the Key bindings page says when one is made.
5. **In edit mode, a focused filmstrip, canvas, or list in the center or the right column** takes the arrows, with ⇧ or ⌥, and Delete and Forward Delete (Spec 11 §2).
6. **A sheet or popover** takes Return and plain Escape.

So ⇧⎋ sends `system clearall` everywhere but in the recorder and an open menu (R1), and only plain Escape closes or cancels. A button that deletes, restores or quits is never a sheet's default, so Return can't press it.

**A text field** is any control that takes typing: text and search fields, date pickers, steppers' fields, combo boxes and the key recorder. A single-line field that acts on Return (the go-to field, a popup or timer row, a rename, a new title, a note) hands the focus back to the window when it acts, so the next Space sends `next`. Search fields and the command bar keep the focus until Escape or a click elsewhere. In a field that edits (in-place text, a rename, a new title, a note, a Settings field), plain Escape discards what was typed and leaves the field; in any other field it leaves the field and keeps the text, apart from the go-to field, which it clears. The top bar says while a field has the focus, and what its keys do (§8).

**Digit entry** is built in (UX spec): with no field focused, an unbound digit key, on the top row or the keypad, goes into the go-to field, and Return fires `cue N` in the live list. Escape, or three seconds without a key, clears the digits, and a held digit counts once. While digits are waiting, Return completes them whatever Return is bound to. Digit entry is off while a sheet or popover is open.

**Fixed keys**, which are not bindings:

- **⌃⌥⌘⎋** (R6) takes every output off the screen that holds the app's window, lock or not. The app registers it as a system-wide hot key, which needs no permission, so it works with the window covered or another app active. At once, before the engine answers, the app raises its main window one level above the outputs on that screen; then it sends `releaseScreen` with that screen, and the engine closes those outputs' windows, reports them `released` (§3) and logs a `screenReleased` note, and the window drops back to its level. If the engine doesn't answer within two seconds, the window stays raised with the not-responding banner (§2), so the way back holds even with the engine stuck. The app remembers the screens it released while it runs, names them with `--released` to each engine it starts (§2), and sends `releaseScreen` again to any engine it connects to. At the app's next launch the outputs come back, unless Settings ▸ Outputs removes them, which it offers (§11; choice U-9).
- **Escape** leaves a field, closes a popover, sheet or the keys overlay, and leaves edit mode. It never sends a command.
- **The app's view actions** are menu items with ⌘-shortcuts (§4): Back to Live ⌘L, Multiviewer ⌥⌘M, Show and Edit ⌘1 and ⌘2. They aren't commands, so they can't be bindings (choice U-13).

**The keys overlay** (Help ▸ Keys, ⌘/, or ? in the top bar) lists every binding as its key, drawn as the current keyboard layout labels it, and its command's plain name (§15), then the fixed keys and ⌥-double-click (§6). It names `system clearall` "Clear all channels (popups stay)" and says that in v1 undo changes the library, never the screens. Escape or a click closes it.

**Safeguards.** Settings ▸ Key bindings and the keys overlay warn when nothing is bound to `next` or to `system clearall` (UX spec). A key bound twice on one device is refused by the engine (Spec 5 §6).

**The command bar**, off by default (`commandBar`), is a field across the top of the center when on. ⌘K focuses it. As the operator types, it shows the canonical form, or the error, from OCore's parser (Spec 2 §4); Return sends the command with source `commandbar` and shows the result or the rejection beside it; ↑ and ↓ recall the last fifty commands of the run. Edits are commands like any other there (Spec 5 §2).

## 10. Edits from show mode

Show mode edits the library in a few places, each through Spec 5's edit path, so the engine runs them, logs them with their inverses, and undo reverses them. All of them are refused under the lock (§12), and edits to masters on a `readOnlyMasters` machine (Spec 5 §7); the app disables them in both cases with the reason. A local copy isn't editable in v1 (Spec 5 §3).

- **In-place text** (S7). Double-click on a text cue, ⌥-double-click, or Edit Text in its menu, opens a field over the cell holding the body of its text write. Return makes a new line; ⌘Return commits, and so does a click outside, which does nothing else: it doesn't fire, select or drag what it lands on. Escape cancels. A commit sends one `edit.cueChannel` for that cue and channel with the new body, keeping the write's other fields, its riders and the channel's attribute writes (Spec 5 §4). The edit shows on the cue's next fire (L3). The field sends nothing until it commits (choice U-12).
- **A pool tile dropped on a cue** (S7) opens a popover of channels with the item's drop channel picked (§6). Return or a click sends one `edit.cueChannel` writing the item on that channel: `write media sha256:…`, keeping the transition and audio-transition riders of what the cue wrote there before and the channel's attribute writes, with the item's default grade as a `grade G once` rider, or none (UX spec: a pool default "inserts an explicit grade write"). The item's trim, loop, gain and sync offset aren't written, since they fall back to its defaults when the write lands (Spec 3 §4). The UX spec's "remembered per kind" is the machine's `dropChannels` (Spec 8 §8). A file dropped from the Finder sends the same edit once its import is done (§6, choice U-8).
- **The grade picker** (S7). Grade for Channel N ▸ on a list header, where the UX spec's grid header had it, sets the grade of the first cue in that list that writes content to channel N: one `edit.cueChannel` giving that write a `grade G track` rider, or removing its grade rider for None (Spec 5 §5).
- **Managing playlists, lists and folders**: the edits of §5, and the media defaults, delete and replace of §6.
- **Deleting media** asks first, naming its uses from `mediaUse`: "Delete "Ocean loop"? It's used by 12 cues, 1 macro and 2 keys; they'll show it missing." Then `media delete HASH` (Spec 8 §6).

**Text fields** in show mode (renames, notes, in-place text, the popover fields) each send one edit when they commit, never while typing; live typing at four sends a second is the text editor's (Spec 5 §5, Spec 11 §5). A commit that can't be sent, because the lock came on or the engine went away, leaves the field open with its text, the reason and Copy. ⌘Q, ⌘2 and Lock Station commit an open field first. When the lock comes on from elsewhere, edit mode closes to show mode on the same cue.

**Undo and redo.** Edit ▸ Undo and Redo send `undo` and `redo` (Spec 5 §8). Their names come from the `history` topic's top groups, made plain from the first command's verb: Undo Rename, Undo Typing, Undo Add, Undo Remove, Undo Move, Undo Duplicate, Undo Delete Media, Undo Restore, or Undo Change, with "(N changes)" for a group of several, and the source when it wasn't this app: "Undo Rename (from OSC)". After an undo or a redo, the window says for three seconds what changed: "Undid the rename of the playlist Sunday". While a text field has the focus, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo its own typing instead (Spec 5 §8), apart from edit mode's text editor (Spec 11 §5). Both are disabled under the lock and while the engine is away. In v1 undo changes the library, never what is on the screens.

**Rejections** come back as a short message in plain words beside what was tried, from the code: `onAir` "It's on air: clear it or switch away first", `archived` "Archived playlists can't be changed", `duplicate` "That name is taken", `operatorLock` "The station is locked", and so on, one sentence for each code of Spec 5 §6 and Spec 8 §7.

## 12. The operator lock and secrets

**What the lock is** (S13). The machine's `operatorLock.enabled`, which persists across launches. While it is on, the engine refuses every edit, every media command but `media rebuild`, every undo and redo of an edit and every restore, from every source (Spec 5 §7), and runtime commands pass. The UX spec's Operator role is this lock in v1.

**In the app**, the lock disables every edit and media command show mode makes (§5, §6 and §10, the cue and list-header menus' edits, imports and drops from the Finder included), edit mode and the Mode menu's Edit, Bind a Key…, Undo and Redo, the restore pickers, New Token…, and every settings page but the lock's own controls. Browsing, firing from the grid and the pool, Make Live, drops of pool tiles on the strip, the transport, the clears, the macros, the popups and timers, stack switches, the keys, Take Snapshot, Reveal in Finder, Show and Copy for the token, Quit, Restart the engine and ⌃⌥⌘⎋ all work. The top bar shows the lock tag.

**Locking** is an edit, `edit machine operatorLock.enabled true`, from any source (choice U-6), refused with `noPasscode` until a passcode is set and with `keychain` while the passcode can't be read, so a station never locks without a way to unlock it. Lock Station (⌃⌘L) sends it, first asking for a passcode when none is set. That edit never joins the undo stack, so undo and redo never lock or unlock the station (§17 corrects Spec 5 §8).

**Unlocking and the passcode** are requests on the local socket (§3), never commands, so a passcode is never in a command, a log line or a snapshot (Spec 5 §7):

| Request | Does | Refused with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `{"type": "passcode", "new": "…", "old": "…"}` | Sets the passcode, or changes it; `old` is needed when one is set | `badPasscode` for a wrong `old`; `badValue` for a passcode under four characters |
| `{"type": "unlock", "passcode": "…"}` | Turns the lock off and writes `machine.json` | `badPasscode` |
| `{"type": "resetPasscode", "authorization": "…"}` | For a forgotten passcode: removes it and turns the lock off, given an administrator's authorization, the base64 of its external form | `notAdmin` |
| `{"type": "token"}` | Answers the API token | |
| `{"type": "newToken"}` | Makes a new token, closes every LAN connection so clients sign in with the new one, and answers it | `operatorLock` |

After five wrong passcodes in a row, counting `unlock` and `passcode`'s `old` together, both are refused with `tooManyAttempts` for 60 seconds. The engine keeps the count in memory and writes it with the passcode in the Keychain on its background thread, so a restart doesn't reset it; a write that fails or waits on a prompt never delays an answer. While a Keychain item can't be read, the requests that need it are refused with `keychain`. Each request but `token` writes a note with the requester's source and no secret: `passcodeSet`, `unlocked`, `unlockFailed`, `passcodeReset`, `tokenRenewed`. Turning the lock off writes `machine.json` and sends a `library` event with cause `lock`; turning it on is an edit like any other. The `engine` topic carries the state either way (§3).

**On disk.** The lock is the engine's: a change to `operatorLock.enabled` that arrives on disk never changes it, since the reload keeps the engine's value and writes it back, with a log line, so editing `machine.json` by hand doesn't unlock the station.

**The forgotten passcode** (S13). Forgot Passcode… asks macOS for an administrator's name and password (Authorization Services, the `system.privilege.admin` right) and sends the authorization to the engine, which checks it before removing the passcode. The app then says the passcode was removed and the station unlocked, and that locking needs a new one.

**The Keychain** (R2). Only the engine reads or writes it: two generic-password items under the service "Project O", with the machine's uuid in their account names, holding the passcode as a salted hash (PBKDF2) with the attempt count, and the API token (32 random bytes, base64url). They are in the login keychain, not the data-protection keychain, which needs an entitlement only a paid team can grant (ADR 0007). The engine reads both at launch (§2) and keeps the passcode hash in memory, so an unlock never waits on a Keychain prompt in the middle of a service. It makes the token at first launch and the passcode item when one is first set.

**The unlock sheet** opens with its passcode field focused, from the lock tag or Unlock Station… (⌃⌘L): the field, Unlock, and Forgot Passcode…. Settings ▸ Roles and lock holds the same on its page (§11). A wrong passcode shakes the field and says so; after five, it says when to try again.

## 13. First run

**The roots.** On a Mac where the app has no roots yet, a sheet asks for the campus name and where to keep the library, with `~/Project O/` as the default, `library/` and `machine/` going inside it. The sheet can instead use a library that is already there, such as one synced from central: the operator picks its folder, which must hold `library.json` (the sheet says what it found otherwise, so a wrong folder never gets a first-run library), and the machine root goes in `~/Project O/machine/`, unless they pick another place; never inside the synced folder. Both places must be on the startup volume, outside Desktop, Documents and Downloads (Spec 8 §2), and short enough for the socket (§2). The app stores both paths (§2) and starts the engine with `--campus`.

**The engine writes the defaults** (C1: the app never writes in the roots). At launch, when the library root has no `library.json`, it writes the first-run library: Spec 6 §9's channels 1 to 3, Presets 1 and 2 and text styles 1 and 2; Spec 1 §2's built-in transitions 1 to 3 (cut, dissolve, fade through a colour); audio transitions 1 to 3 (cut, crossfade, fade through silence); all with the fixture library's values (Spec 1 §10); empty grades and macros; an empty media index; and `media/` with `imports/` as the default import folder and `fonts/`. When the machine root has no `machine.json`, it writes:

| Document | First run |
| --- | --- |
| `machine.json` | A new uuid, the campus name from `--campus`, and Spec 1 §9's values, with the Mac's time zone, `libraryMode` `writable` (choice U-10, E-2), `dropChannels` as Spec 8 §8 has them, and `mediaRoot` the path from the machine root to the library root's `media/`: `../library/media` in the default layout |
| `outputs.json` | One output per DeckLink output port, then per screen but the one `--operator-screen` names (S3), below |
| `stacks.json` | Stack 1, Main (channels 1 and 2), output 1's default; stack 2, Confidence (channels 2 and 3), on no output; then one stack per further output with channels 1 and 2, output N's being stack N + 1, labelled like its output (Spec 6 §9) |
| `audio-outputs.json` | Audio output 1, the system's default output device at first run, by its UID, with its first two channels; then one per DeckLink output's embedded audio, which no channel feeds until someone routes one |
| `keys.json` | §9's defaults |
| Others | `inputs.json` and `clocks.json` empty, no playlists, no macros |

The engine also makes the API token (§12). A machine root with `machine.json` but a library root without `library.json`, or the other way round, gets only the missing side.

**Which screens become outputs** (S3). Every DeckLink output port, then every screen except the one holding the app's window, numbered in that order: ports in the order the driver lists them, then screens left to right, top to bottom where two share a left edge. So output 1 is the main look. Labels are the devices' names. A screen's output takes its current mode, progressive and full range. A DeckLink port starts at 1920 by 1080, progressive, limited range, free-running, in the displays' rate family: 60 beside 60 Hz screens, 59.94 beside 59.94 Hz screens or with no screen output (choice U-11, Spec 4 §2). Every output's switch transition is 1 (cut) and its blackout transition 2 (dissolve), the UX spec's cut for switches and fade for blackout. A Mac with one screen and no card gets no outputs, and neither does one whose screens are mirrored, which macOS reports as one screen: stack 1 shows in the preview, and the top bar says why (§8).

**The first window** names each new output ("Output 1 is DELL U2720Q"), with Settings ▸ Outputs a click away, beside the UX spec's line that these defaults are the operator's to rename, number and extend, until dismissed.

## 14. Restores, saving and the engine's state

**A list's versions** (Spec 9 §6). Restore a Version… on a list opens a sheet of its kept versions from the `versions` query, newest first, each with when it was kept, its title and how many cues it had. Restore asks: "Restore version 16 of "Great Is Thy Faithfulness", kept Nov 1 at 09:12, with 14 cues? The current version is kept, and Undo puts it back." Then `system restore list N version V`. A list on air takes it at each cue's next fire, and the sheet says so.

**Snapshots** (Spec 9 §6). Restore a Snapshot… (File menu, or Settings ▸ Startup and backup) opens the snapshots from the `snapshots` query, newest first, each with its time, its reason and its size, and the campus and machine when another machine took it. Restore asks: "Replace the library, and this machine's playlists, key bindings and macros, with the copies from Nov 8 at 08:59? Outputs, stacks and settings stay as they are. A snapshot of now is taken first, and Undo puts it back." On a `readOnlyMasters` machine it names only this machine's playlists, key bindings and macros (Spec 9 §6, G-7). Then `system restore snapshot "ID"`, which runs on the engine's worker, so the sheet shows it working until its result. A refusal is shown with its reason in plain words: the renumbered objects for `conflict`, the channel on air for `onAir`, the disk for `diskFull`.

**The saving tag** in the top bar reads the `saving` topic: amber "Unsaved (3)" when documents have waited more than five seconds to be written, amber "Disk low", and red "Disk full: changes not saved" with the log in memory. A click lists what is waiting, with Reveal in Finder for the machine folder.

**The last state.** When the engine has put the last state back this run (the `engine` topic's `lastState`), a banner says "Picked up where the engine stopped, at 09:41. Video started again from the top." It names what didn't come back, and says that timers came back unset (Spec 6 §6), with Set Timers Again, which sets each timer to its last value from the `timers` topic and starts those that were running. When a relaunch couldn't put it back, the banner says why, from the reason (Spec 9 §2), and offers "Fire 3 Verse 2 again", built from the last `live` event the app saw as an ordinary fire. When the setting is on and a launch skipped it, the banner says why: the last run ended cleanly, the state was more than two hours old, or it had already been put back once. Dismissed, it stays away for that run (§4).

**The engine tag** lists the standing badges in plain words: "The Mac is hot and may slow down" for `thermal`, "Memory is low" for `memory`, "The engine recovered from a stall" for `watchdog`, "The graphics card was reset" for `gpu`, "Running on the backup clock" for `clock`, "The Keychain can't be read: remote control and unlocking are off" for `keychain`.

## 15. Accessibility and localization

**VoiceOver** works from the first build, as the planning doc's decisions require. Every control has a label and its state ("Channel 2 Foreground, text, "Great is Thy faithfulness", locked, in stacks 1 and 2"). The grid is a collection whose cells say their number, label, section, trigger, and "live" or "armed". Every change of the live cue is announced, with its source when it wasn't the app ("Auto: Chorus b", "From OSC: Verse 2"), and so are a trigger arming ("Auto in 8 seconds"), an output that stops presenting, and a write a lock blocked or that stepped on a transition. The strip's meters and transition bars are left out of the spoken order.

**Keyboard navigation.** Every action is reachable without the mouse. The menus hold the app's actions; Tab and ⇧Tab move the focus between regions and controls, the top bar's buttons included, whatever the system's keyboard-navigation setting. Arrows, Space and Return belong to the show keys (§9), so in a focused list, tree, grid or tile set ⌥ with the arrows moves the selection; ⌥Return or ⌥Space presses or activates whatever has the focus, a button, a tile, a cue or a row; and ⌃Return opens the focused item's menu, which holds what a right-click offers. A bound key always does its binding, apart from the arrows and Delete in edit mode's focused editors (Spec 11 §2).

**Colour is never alone.** Every state shown in colour also has a shape or a word: the four chip states differ in fill, outline, stripe and strike; the live cue has a border and a "Live" tag; browsing says "Browsing"; an output that isn't presenting has a hollow shape and its state in a word. Increase Contrast thickens borders and outlines.

**Reduce Motion** shows the transition bar's end state instead of running it, turns the unlock field's shake into a red outline, turns each flash into a steady highlight for a second, and keeps the remote dot lit while remote commands keep arriving.

**Localization.** Every string shown is in the app's string catalog, English only in v1, and built from whole sentences with placeholders rather than pieces joined in code, so another language can reorder them. Counts use the catalog's plural variants and lists the system's list formatter ("12 cues, 1 macro and 2 keys"). A command's plain name, in the keys overlay and elsewhere, comes from a table of one sentence with placeholders per command object (Spec 2 §3), and a command without one shows its canonical text. Keys are drawn as the current keyboard layout labels them, though matched by position (§9). Numbers, durations, dates and times use the system's formatters, except where a setting fixes the format, such as `[Time]`'s.

## 16. The areas, and what they change on main

**A new area for the engine side** (choice U-1). Spec 2 §9's API has no server on `main` and no area owns one: the engine is driven over OSC, and Areas 8, 9, 11 and 12 each add topics, queries or requests to it. So Area 19, **Engine API and first run** (branch `area/api`), builds §2's local socket, LAN listener, Bonjour and Mach channel (proving the Mach call first), the token's making and reading for the LAN, the subscriptions, queries and results of §3 with the topics the earlier areas publish, the `ping`, `stop` and `releaseScreen` requests, the meters, the preview surfaces, three per stack, and the channel pictures with their aux pass, three per channel, each with their frame messages, the first-run writer of §13 with output detection and `--campus`, the relaunch rule with `--relaunch` and `--released` (§2, a Spec 9 §4 change), the `preview` topic, and a client in the soak scenario that holds the Mach channel (§18). It also builds the engine's process behaviour: the lock file with the process id, the console log trimmed in place, the activity and sleep assertions, the output windows as non-activating panels on every Space, and display outputs that wait for their own screen: `screen(for:)` in `EngineProcess.swift` falls back to the next screen today, and that goes. It is engine-only, so it needs no bench run, and it starts after Area 12. Until it lands, Areas 8, 9, 11 and 12 publish their events and answer their queries and requests through the engine's own publisher, which their tests read in-process (a Spec 2 §9 addition, §17).

**Area 13** (branch `area/show`) builds the app: the Xcode project depending on the root package, starting, finding and restarting the engine with the curtains (§2), the main window and every region (§4–§8), the keys (§9), the edits of §10, the notes, `view.json`, and §14's sheets and tags, with VoiceOver and the string catalog from the start (§15). It starts after Area 19 and after the bench run passes (Spec 4 §15).
