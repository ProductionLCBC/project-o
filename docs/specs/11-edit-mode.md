## 1. Purpose and scope

Edit mode is where a library is authored: the cues of a list, the text of a song, the stacks and channels of a machine, the presets that give text and pictures their place, and the small objects cues refer to (grades, transitions, audio transitions, text styles, macros). It is the UX spec's "Edit mode" sections made exact, with the scope doc's answers (E1–E9, C4, R5) and the clickable demo's cue inspector. Every change it makes is a Spec 5 edit, run by the engine, logged with its inverse and undone with `undo`. Nothing here is new behaviour on air: edit mode changes documents, and Spec 5 §2 says when each change shows.

**In scope:** the edit-mode frame (its regions, entry and exit, the pages, keys, the lock, editing what is live); the two queries the editors need from the engine (`render` and `uses`); the Cues page (the list's cues, the cue inspector, its palette and raw command view, multi-cue editing); the text editor and its paste rules; the stack editor; the channel sheet; the preset editor, with text styles authored in a box's properties; the small editors for grades, transitions, audio transitions, text styles and macros; their accessibility; what Areas 14 and 10 build, in the engine and the app.

**Out of scope:** show mode and settings (Spec 10), which this spec only joins where they open edit mode; the slide designer, timelines, Timecode triggers and chase (v2); builds, which belong to designed slides (v2); live inputs and their freeze (v1.5); the campus-override view of a preset and campus variants (cut by the planning doc, v1.5 with library mode); editing local copies (L2, v1.5 with library mode); macro guards (Spec 2 §7, v1.5); creating transitions (the transition editor the planning doc cut); the history view (v1.5).

**Rules every page follows:**

- **Edits only.** A control sends one Spec 5 edit, or one group of them, when it commits; the app writes nothing in the roots (C1). The page shows what the engine answers: a `library` event (Spec 5 §2) makes the page ask again for what it shows (Spec 10 §3), the Text page patching only the chunks the event names (§5), and a rejection puts the control back with the reason under it, as Spec 10 §11's fields do.
- **Fields commit** on Return or when they lose focus, sliders and colour wells when the drag ends, pickers when a choice is made. Only the text editor's chunks send while typing, at most four times a second, as Spec 5 §5 folds it; every other text field, a popup command's text included, sends once, on commit (Spec 10 §10).
- **Adding and choosing** (Adam, 2026-11-24). A list that things are added to has a + row at its foot that adds one, so nothing is added from a menu alone; a menu that adds or applies something lists only its choices, never its own name among them; and every slider has a field beside it that takes a typed value.
- **Only what exists.** Pickers offer the objects this machine has, by number and label, and each type's own values: a box's transition picker offers transitions of type cut or dissolve, the box transitions v1 draws (Spec 1 §2), and an attribute whose tracking is off offers no "from here on" for anything new (Spec 3 §3 rule 7, §4).
- **Read-only, with the reason,** wherever the engine would refuse: a local copy (Spec 5 §3, `notInV1`); a library object (a list, a channel, a preset, a text style, a grade, a transition, an audio transition or a portable macro) on a `readOnlyMasters` machine (Spec 5 §7, `readOnlyLibrary`); a protected field (Spec 5 §6); and a list whose number another list shares when edits by that number reach the other one, which comes first (Spec 7 §2); that page offers to renumber the other (§4). Operator lock closes edit mode altogether (§2).
- **AppKit**, as CLAUDE.md divides the toolkits: the pages are editors, so they are AppKit, apart from the small editors' simple forms, which may be SwiftUI.

## 2. The edit-mode frame

**One window, six pages** (UX spec). Edit mode keeps the main window and lays it out its own way (choice A-16, as revised on 2026-11-22 from Adam's drawing of the frame, §14): panels on a darker ground, 4 points apart.

| Region | Contents | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top bar | Show and Edit, what is being edited, the page picker and a short status (below) | 44 points high |
| Browser | On the Cues and Text pages, three tabs: Playlists, Library and Media (below); on the other pages, the page's own list (below) | 260 points wide, the full height under the top bar |
| Workspace | The page's editing space; on the Cues page, the cue grid (§4) | The rest |
| Monitors | Preview over Program (below) | A quarter of the window's width, from 300 to 380 points |
| Inspector | The fields of what the page has selected, in columns (§4, §8), on the pages that have one | 250 points high, under the workspace and the monitors |

These are the sizes a window opens at. The gaps beside the Browser, the monitors and the inspector drag to resize them (Adam, 2026-11-24): the Browser from 200 to 420 points wide, the monitors from 260 to 560, the inspector from 180 to 420 points high, the workspace keeping at least 360 by 200; `view.json` keeps the sizes. Show mode keeps Spec 10 §4's regions, drawn in this frame's look (Spec 10 §4). Its left column, media pool panel and right column aren't shown in edit mode, where the Browser, the monitors and the Program panel's controls stand in for them. Edit mode draws in show mode's palette (Spec 10 §4), with three more fills of the same family, which show mode's panels use too: #060708 between the panels, #282C34 for the chosen part of a control and for a strong button, and #08090B for the well of a field.

The pages are picked in the top bar's page picker, and in the View menu with ⌥⌘1 to ⌥⌘6:

**Where a page's parts sit** (Adam, 2026-11-24, after trying the Stacks, Channels and Presets pages). Each page lays the frame out for its own work. The Cues and Text pages keep the Browser, since they edit the list it picks. The Stacks, Channels, Presets and Objects pages put their own list in its place, as wide and as resizable: the page's objects by number and label, the selected one highlighted and the live dot on what is on air, with a + row at the list's foot that adds one, and on each row a ⋯ menu with what else can be done to it (Duplicate, Renumber…, Delete). The Preview monitor shows the engine's picture of what the page has selected, its picker naming what that page needs (§6 to §8), and Program keeps the show running on every page. The fields go where the page has room for them: the Cues and Presets pages keep the inspector under the workspace and the monitors; the Stacks and Channels pages have none, and set their fields in tall columns in the workspace (§6, §7). §5 to §9 say where each part of their page sits.

`view.json` (Spec 10 §4) also keeps the last page, each page's selection, the Browser's tab and its media view, and the cue grid's zoom and its folded and hidden rows (§4), so edit mode reopens where it was left.

**The top bar** in edit mode:

- **Left:** Show and Edit, one control with Edit chosen (⌘1, ⌘2); then what the page edits. For the Cues and Text pages that is where the list came from and its title, "Sunday 9:00 › Amazing Grace", or "Library › Amazing Grace"; for the other pages, the selected object's number and label. While a text field has the focus, what its keys do follows it, in Spec 10 §8's words.
- **Center:** the page picker: Cues, Text, Stacks, Channels, Presets, Objects. Text is disabled until Area 10 lands (§11). Beside it, the Live toggle (below).
- **Right:** the time of day; the saving pill, which reads "Saved" while nothing waits to be written and is otherwise show mode's saving tag, with its click (Spec 10 §14); and the engine pill, a green dot and "Engine" while the engine is connected, every output is presenting and no badge stands. Before them, an output that isn't presenting or is blacked out shows as its show-mode dot, and each of show mode's tags shows while it applies (Spec 10 §8), so nothing show mode's bar reports is missed here. A click on the engine pill lists every output as show mode's dots do, each with its details, its Stack ▸ menu and, for an output on a screen, Release This Screen or Put Back on This Screen (Spec 10 §8). While the engine isn't connected the pill is hollow and red, and the banner says why (Spec 10 §2).
- While another app is active the whole bar says so, as in show mode.

**Live** (Adam, 2026-11-24), one toggle beside the page picker, says what moving the cue grid's selection does. Off, which edit mode always opens with, it only selects: nothing goes to air. On, each cue the selection moves onto, by the arrow keys or a click, is fired with `state` where Fire would fire it (§4): its list's state before it is taken up and the cue fires (Spec 3 §5), so the outputs show what its thumbnail shows, wherever in the list the editor goes. A change can then be checked on the outputs as it is made. ⇧ and ⌘ with a click or an arrow only select; the toggle is drawn in the live red while it is on.

**The Browser** stands where show mode's left column does and holds what the Cues and Text pages pick from. The other pages put their own list in its place (above).

- **Playlists** and **Library** are show mode's two windows (Spec 10 §5), one to a tab, with their trees, search, menus and drags, and the live dot on the live playlist and item. A click on a linked item or on a library list makes it the list the Cues and Text pages edit, which the Cues page shows at once when it is the page in view; a double-click opens the Cues page on it from any page.
- **Media** is the media pool (Spec 10 §6) at the Browser's width: the search field; the filter menu, with show mode's kinds, its folders, Used in This List, Replaced and the sort; the count of the items shown; and Grid, two tiles to a row, or List, a small tile beside the item's name with its kind and duration under it. Items drag onto whatever takes media: a cell of the cue grid (§4), a sample or a preview's item (§8, §9). Files dropped on the tab are imported as on the pool (Spec 10 §6), with the jobs and the refusals listed at the tab's foot until dismissed. An item's menu has Defaults…, Replace with…, Reveal in Finder and Delete…. A double-click and Fire to… are show mode's alone, so nothing in the Browser goes to air.

**The monitors.**

- **Preview** is the engine's picture of what the page has selected (choice A-1), outlined in the selection's blue: on the Cues page, the selected cue's projected state (§4), without the channels whose rows are hidden. Its header holds the stack picker, and a pill while channels are hidden ("Hiding 2 channels"), which is show mode's preview stack (Spec 10 §7): one choice for both modes, starting on the thumbnail stack.
- **Program** keeps the show running while editing (UX spec). It shows the live picture of the same stack, from its preview surface (Spec 10 §2), outlined in the live red while an output shows it, with a pill naming those outputs, "On air · Out 1, 2"; when none does, the outline is plain and the pill says "Preview only". A blacked-out stack is dimmed and labelled as in show mode's preview. Under the picture, a line gives the live position in the top bar's words (Spec 10 §8) and, in amber, the armed countdown; for three seconds it says instead why a command did nothing (Spec 10 §7). Under that: the go-to field, which takes digit entry as in show mode and fires cue N of the live list, whatever the page shows, showing the live list's title while digits wait (Spec 10 §7, §9); Next (`next`), disabled while nothing is live (choice U-15); and Clear All (`system clearall`), red and set apart.

Next and the go-to field fire within what is live; Fire, on the Cues page, is the one control that fires a cue of any list (§4). Nothing in edit mode applies an edit to live: a change shows when Spec 5 §2 says it does.

**Opening it.** Mode ▸ Edit (⌘2) or Edit in the top bar opens the last page on what show mode had in hand: the list the grid browses, from its playlist or the Library window, and the selected cue, else the live cue if it is in that list, else the first. The pages keep where a list came from, which Fire (§4) and leaving use. Show mode also opens it on something:

- a double-click on a cue that isn't a text cue, or Edit Cue in a cue's menu, opens the Cues page on that cue (Spec 10 §6);
- Open in Text Editor, in a list header's menu or a list's menu in the Library window, opens the Text page on that list. It is named apart from the cue menu's Edit Text, which edits one cue in place in show mode (Spec 10 §10);
- Edit on a macro tile opens Objects on that macro (Spec 10 §7);
- Edit in Stack Editor on an output in Settings ▸ Outputs opens the Stacks page on that output (Spec 10 §11).

The app notes `editMode` with `on` true when edit mode opens and false when it closes (Spec 10 §3).

**Leaving it.** Show in the top bar, Escape with nothing else to close (below), or Mode ▸ Show (⌘1) returns to show mode on the cue the Cues or Text page has selected: the grid browses where its list came from, the playlist or the Library window, and scrolls to it, firing nothing. There is no Done button. An open field commits first (Spec 10 §10). If the engine refuses that commit, edit mode stays on the field with the reason, and a second try leaves without it.

**The lock** closes edit mode (UX spec, S13). While operator lock is on, Mode ▸ Edit, Edit in the top bar and every entry above are disabled with the reason (Spec 10 §12). When it comes on from elsewhere, edit mode closes to show mode on the same cue, and an open field's unsent text is offered in a sheet with Copy, since the engine would refuse it.

**Keys** follow Spec 10 §9, with one addition (choice A-3). In edit mode a focused cue grid, canvas, or list in the workspace or the inspector takes the arrow keys, with ⇧ or ⌥, Return, and Delete and Forward Delete, as a text field takes typing: the arrows move the selection in the grid, ⇧ extending it across cues (§4), or nudge the selected box on a canvas (§8); Return opens the selected cell's content (§4); and Delete does what §4 and §8 give it, asking first wherever its menu would. The Browser keeps show mode's keys. So stepping through cues or nudging a box never fires `next` or `previous` (in Live, the arrows fire the cue they land on, above), while every other binding still fires: Space sends `next`, and ⇧⎋ clears all everywhere but the key recorder and an open menu. The text editor is a text field (§5). Escape leaves a field first, then closes a popover, a sheet or the keys overlay, then clears waiting digits, then leaves edit mode.

**What is live.** Edit mode edits what the Browser and the pages select, live or not; the live list, cue, stack and output carry the live dot. When an edit shows on air is Spec 5 §2's:

- a cue's commands and trigger at the cue's next fire (L3), but at once in a box at +1 to +3 on air, such as the confidence screen's, which shows what will happen (Spec 5 §2, Spec 6 §3);
- a list's metadata, and a cue's section and note, from the next frame wherever a field box or a context value shows them (P5, P12);
- a preset, a grade, a transition's timing, a stack or an output from the next frame (choice E-6).

The first edit in a run to something on air says so in the inspector's notice line (below), naming where it shows: "On air on outputs 1 and 2: this shows at once."

**Undo.** Edit ▸ Undo and Redo send `undo` and `redo`, named from the `history` topic, as in show mode (Spec 10 §10). One step is one group: a split, a merge, a paste, a multi-cue change, a drag on a canvas, a second of typing (Spec 5 §5). The text editor's ⌘Z is the library's undo while the last change is the editor's own (choice A-5, §5); in any other text field it undoes the field's own typing until the field commits (Spec 5 §8, Spec 10 §10).

**Rejections and warnings.** The inspector's header keeps one notice line for what the control last used has to say, since its columns have no room under each control. A rejected edit puts its control back, outlined in red, and the line says why in plain words, with Spec 10 §10's sentence for its code. An applied edit's `warnings` (Spec 5 §6) show there in yellow until the object is next edited. The line's tooltip and VoiceOver carry its whole text. A page that is read-only says why in its workspace's header (§1).

**Other clients.** A second station on the LAN, OSC, the command bar or a cue that holds an edit may change what a page shows. The page takes the change from the `library` event, except in a field that has the focus, which keeps its text until it commits; the engine's last writer wins (Spec 5 §2). A page whose object is deleted under it says "Deleted", with Undo while that delete is the history's top group.

## 3. What the editors ask of the engine

Edit mode reads the documents Spec 10 §3's queries return (`catalog`, `list`, `media`, `thumbnails`, `fonts`) and the preview surfaces (Spec 10 §2), and sends edits. It adds two queries (choices A-1 and A-9): `render`, so that what an editor shows is what the engine draws, and `uses`, for the "used by" the UX spec asks of every object. Like every query, each is answered with its request's `id` as `{"id": …, "type": "answer", …}`, and refused with Spec 10 §3's rejected result (a Spec 2 §9 addition, §12). `render` answers with a file on this Mac, so it is the local socket's only, refused on the LAN with `localOnly`; `uses` is local and LAN alike.

**`render`** draws a picture for an editor's canvas and answers with a file, as thumbnails are files (Spec 10 §3):

```json
{ "id": 61, "type": "query", "what": "render", "canvas": "inspector",
  "subject": { "kind": "cue", "listUuid": "a9f0…", "cue": "11aa…", "stack": 1 }, "width": 1280 }
{ "id": 61, "type": "answer", "file": "thumbnails/render/c3-inspector-61.png", "width": 1280, "height": 720,
  "overflow": [], "fallbackFonts": [] }
```

| `subject.kind` | Fields | Draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cue` | `listUuid` (or `local`), `cue`, `stack`, optionally `hide` | The cue's projected state on that stack, Spec 3 §7's cue view, as the thumbnail job draws it (Spec 4 §10); the channels `hide` lists by number draw nothing (§4's hidden rows; a number no channel has is ignored) |
| `layout` | `preset`, `layout` (`"*"` or a stack number), `stack` (for `*` only), `sample` | The layout with the sample standing in for the slot's channel (below): a stack's own layout at that stack's size and number, the `*` layout at the size and number of the stack `stack` names |
| `grade` | `grade` (or null), `media` | The item's poster or still through the grade, or as it is when `grade` is null, over black |

- **The sample** is what a channel could hold: `content`, one text write's `fields` (`{"kind": "text", "fields": {…}}`), one media item (`{"kind": "media", "ref": "sha256:…"}`), the engine's test pattern (`{"kind": "pattern"}`, Adam, 2026-11-24) or nothing (`{"kind": "empty"}`); `next`, up to three more contents for boxes at +1 to +3; and optionally `listUuid` and `cue`, naming where the sample came from. Field boxes draw the text's fields, falling back to that list's metadata (Spec 6 §3); pixel boxes draw the media as its poster or still, or the test pattern, which the engine draws itself at the stack's size, so a box's fit, crop and place read at once: a grid at each tenth of the frame, the centre lines and a circle, the action-safe and title-safe areas (93% and 90%), and a row of colour bars; it ships in the engine's code, never as a file in the repository; static boxes draw their text, their context values reading that cue (Spec 6 §5); fallback boxes follow the content's kind as on air (Spec 4 §9). Without `listUuid` and `cue`, metadata and context values draw nothing.
- **Size.** `width` is 160 to 1920 pixels. The height follows the stack's size, or the item's for a grade. A stack's size is that of the output showing it, else of an output whose default stack it is, else 1920 by 1080, the size its stack pass and preview use at that moment (a Spec 4 §5 addition, §12), so a picture's shape is the screen's.
- **What it reports.** For a layout, `overflow` lists each text box whose sample text overflows (Spec 6 §4) as `{ "box": 2, "offset": 0, "linesCut": 2 }`, and `fallbackFonts` each box whose font fell back to `fallbackFont`, with the font's name. Boxes are numbered by their place in the layout, from 0.
- **When.** The engine draws it on the thumbnail job's background queue, with the thumbnail job's code, ahead of thumbnails, and waits as that job does while the render thread is skipping aux passes (Spec 4 §10). A request names its `canvas`, up to 16 letters and digits, scoped to its connection, which may use eight canvases; a newer request for the same canvas replaces one still waiting, which is answered `{"superseded": true}`. A drag on a canvas therefore never queues a picture per pixel moved.
- **The files.** `machine/thumbnails/render/`, named by connection, canvas and request `id` (`c3-inspector-61.png`), keeping each canvas's newest two; the folder is emptied at launch, and a connection's files when it closes. Nothing else reads them.
- **Errors.** An unknown list, cue, preset, layout, grade or media id is `unknownObject`; a width outside 160 to 1920, a canvas name that isn't letters and digits or is longer than 16, or a connection's ninth canvas is `badValue`; a media item whose file is missing draws nothing for the pixel boxes, and the answer says `missing: true`.

**`uses`** lists what refers to a numbered object, counted over both roots' documents on this machine, as `mediaUse` counts media (Spec 8 §7):

```json
{ "id": 62, "type": "query", "what": "uses", "object": "preset", "preset": 3 }
{ "id": 62, "type": "answer", "more": false,
  "counts": { "channels": 1, "stacks": 3, "presets": 0, "boxes": 0, "outputs": 0, "audiooutputs": 0, "cues": 0, "macros": 0, "keys": 0, "media": 0, "machine": 0 },
  "uses": { "channels": [ { "channel": 2, "field": "defaultPreset" } ],
            "stacks": [ { "stack": 1, "slot": 2 }, { "stack": 2, "field": "defaultPreset" }, { "stack": 3, "slot": 1, "fallback": true } ] } }
```

- **Objects:** `channel`, `preset`, `textstyle`, `grade`, `transition`, `audiotransition`, `macro`, `stack`, `output` and `audiooutput`. Media keep `mediaUse` and lists `listUse` (Spec 10 §3).
- **References:** a channel's default preset, transitions and audio outputs; a stack's default preset and its slots' channels and presets; for a preset, also the slots that reach it by fallback (Spec 6 §2), marked `fallback`; a preset's layout for a stack; an output's default stack, transitions and key-fill partner, and the audio outputs that embed into an output; a box's style (`fromStyle`), transition and grade; a media item's default grade and channel; the commands, riders, guards and held edits of cues and macros, and the commands of key bindings and scheduler entries; the machine settings' startup and shutdown macros, thumbnail stack and drop channels.
- **Kinds and entries.** `channels`, `stacks`, `presets`, `boxes`, `outputs`, `audiooutputs`, `cues`, `macros`, `keys` (key bindings and scheduler entries), `media` and `machine`. A channel, stack, output or audio-output entry carries its number and the `field` or slot that refers; a preset entry its `preset` and `layout`; a box entry its preset, layout and place; a cue entry `listUuid` (or `local`), `list`, `cue`, its number and its label; a macro entry its `macro` and the command's place; a key entry its `device` and `key`, or a scheduler entry's place; a media entry its `media` id and `field`; a machine entry its `field`.
- **Limits:** `counts` covers everything, per kind; the entries stop at 500 in all, with `more` true beyond. The engine answers from its loaded documents, so the answer is as fresh as the last `library` event.

## 4. The Cues page

**The list.** The page edits one list: the one chosen in the Browser, a list in its Library tab or a linked item in its Playlists tab (§2). A local item's list opens read-only with "Local copies are edited from v1.5" (L2). The workspace's header names the list, its cue count and its version, and holds the zoom slider (below), Open in Text Editor (§5) and Renumber… (`edit list N renumber M`, or Swap for a taken number). Edits name a list by its number, so when two lists share one they reach the list that comes first (Spec 7 §2): that list's page says "Another list shares number 57", and the other's opens read-only, offering to renumber the first, after which each has a number of its own.

**The cue grid** (choice A-2, as revised on 2026-11-22, §14) fills the workspace: the list's cues across, in order, and the channels down, read from left to right as the list plays, as a video editor's timeline is. It takes the place of the filmstrip, the canvas and the channel rows this page had before. From the top:

- **The section band:** over each run of cues that share a section, the section's name under a rule of its colour (Spec 10 §6's palette). A click on a band selects its run's cues, ⇧ and ⌘ extending the selection as on a header, and a band drags as its headers do (Moving, below) (Adam, 2026-11-24).
- **The cue headers,** one per cue. On one line, its number, the live dot on the live cue, and its trigger as Spec 10 §6 words it ("Go", "+8 s", "adv 12 s"). Under that, the cue's thumbnail on the thumbnail stack, the engine's file (Spec 10 §6), which stands where a title would. Under the thumbnail, the label, on one line. The live cue's header is underlined in red; a selected cue's is underlined in blue, and its column is washed in blue. After the last cue, a + header adds a cue at the end.
- **The channel rows,** one per channel in number order, then one for each channel number the list's cues write that no channel has (E7), named "No channel 7". At a row's left are its number, its label, a control that folds it to a thin strip, and an eye that hides the channel from the Preview monitor's picture alone, as a timeline hides a track (Adam, 2026-11-24): its cells dim, the monitor's header says how many are hidden ("Hiding 2 channels"), and Program, the outputs and the thumbnails are untouched. Hidden rows are kept by channel number for every list, in `view.json`, as folded ones are. This column, the band and the headers stay in place while the grid scrolls. At first the rows of the channels the list writes content to are open and the rest are folded; a row the operator folds or opens stays so for every list, by channel number, in `view.json`. Nothing in the grid adds a channel: channels are made in the Channels page (§7).

**A cell** is one cue on one channel. It shows which of the UX spec's three states the cue has there, from the app's own fold of the list (Spec 10 §6):

- **Write:** a tile. For media, the pool tile with the item's name along its foot, and the missing overlay when the item is missing or deleted (Spec 10 §6). For text, the first lines of its body on black, or its first line alone, as the channel's `textThumbnail` says (Spec 1 §3). For a slide or an input, its kind and reference, not editable in v1. A badge in the tile's corner gives the transition rider's duration, and a mark beside it says the write carries other riders. When the next cue holds the channel, a short bar leaves the tile toward it.
- **Hold,** the default: a bar through the cell while the channel holds something there, and nothing while it holds nothing.
- **Clear:** the bar ends at a cap in the cell's middle, with the word "Clear".
- **Attribute writes on a held or cleared cell** show as a mark on the bar, or beside the cap, since such a cue changes how the channel plays without changing what it holds. The mark's tooltip lists them, and so does the inspector.
- In a folded row a write is a strip of its tile, a clear its cap and a hold its bar.

**Zoom.** The header's slider sets the width of a cue's column, from 72 to 480 points, 140 at first, and a pinch on the grid does the same. The rows' tiles keep their shape, so the whole grid grows and shrinks as a timeline does: zoomed out, a long list shows whole as narrow strips; zoomed in, each tile reads. Below 96 points a header drops its trigger and its label, and a tile its name, its text and its badge, which the tooltip and the inspector still give. The zoom is one setting for every list, in `view.json`.

**Selecting.** The selection is one cue or several, and one row: a channel's, or the header row. A click on a cell selects its cue and its channel. A click on a header selects its cue and the header row, and the inspector keeps the channel it showed. ⇧-click selects a range of cues and ⌘-click adds or removes one: several cues selected is multi-cue editing (below). With the grid focused, ← and → move to the cue before and after, ⇧ with them extends the selection, and ↑ and ↓ move through the rows, the header row at the top (§2). The grid scrolls to keep the selection in view, and the Preview monitor and the inspector show it.

- **Moving.** Headers drag to a new place: `edit list L cue C move K`. A drag that starts on a selected header, or on a band, moves every selected cue, in their order, as one group. Move Earlier and Move Later in the menu do the same by keyboard.
- **A header's menu:** Fire; New Cue After; Duplicate (`… duplicate`); Duplicate Section (`… section duplicate`); Move Earlier; Move Later; Delete (`… remove`, several as one group); Open in Text Editor, on this cue's chunk. A cell's menu starts with Write…, or Edit Content… on a write, then Clear and Hold (below), then the header's items.
- **Delete.** With a channel's row selected, Delete or Forward Delete returns the selected cells to Hold, as the three-way control does (below). With the header row selected it deletes the selected cues, as the menu's Delete does. Deleting cues asks first only when the cues hold edits, or include the live cue or the armed one (choice A-11); otherwise undo is the way back.
- **A new cue** holds every channel, has a Go trigger, an empty label and note, and the section of the cue before it: `edit list L insert cues K {…}` with a uuid the app makes, so a group can name the cue it inserts (Spec 5 §4), and the page selects it.
- **An empty list** shows, with its + header, "No cues yet. Add one with +, or paste the song's text in the text editor" (§5).

**Fire** (choice A-13), in a header's menu and the inspector's header, sends what a click on that cue sends where the page's list came from (Spec 10 §6): `item N cue M` in its playlist, after `playlist "TITLE"` when that isn't loaded, or `list N cue M` from the Library window. The inspector's header says which ("Fires in Sunday"). As in show mode, it is disabled for a playlist its title doesn't load ("Rename to fire") and a list its number doesn't reach ("Renumber to fire"). A refire runs the whole cue again: its media restarts and its timers and macros run again (Spec 3 §4). Fire is the one control that fires a cue of any list; Next and the go-to field fire within what is live (§2).

**The Preview monitor** (§2) is the engine's picture of the selected cue's projected state (Spec 3 §7), from `render` with `subject.kind` `cue` at the monitor's width in pixels (§3), on the stack its picker names. It is asked for again after each `library` event that names the list or anything it draws. A click on it fires nothing.

**Drops.** A media item dragged from the Browser onto a cell writes it there: the same `edit.cueChannel` Spec 10 §10 sends for a drop on a cue, keeping the transition and audio-transition riders and the attribute writes, with the item's default grade as a `grade G once` rider. The cell under the pointer is outlined while it would take the drop. A file from the Finder dropped on a cell is imported, then written, as on a cue in show mode (Spec 10 §6, choice U-8), without the channel popover, since the cell names the channel. The headers and a "No channel" row take no drop.

**The inspector** shows the selected cue in four columns. Its header says "Cue 3 of 14", with the live dot on the live cue; then the notice line (§2); then where Fire fires; ‹ and › (Previous Cue, Next Cue), which move the selection, never the pointer; and Fire.

**The Cue column:** label, section (a combo box offering the list's sections and E3's names) and note, each an `edit.set` on commit. A section carries forward in the editor alone (Adam, 2026-11-24): set on one cue, it is set too on the cues after it whose section was the same as that cue's, up to the first that differs, all as one group, so a band is set once and runs on until a later cue has another. With several cues selected it is set on those alone. The format keeps one section per cue (Spec 1 §4), so moving a cue moves its section with it.

**The Trigger column.** Go, or Time after previous (seconds, to a tenth); Timecode shows, disabled, as v2. Advance after (seconds, or none). Each is an `edit.set` of `trigger` or `advanceAfterMs`. When this cue's Time and the previous cue's advance-after are both set, or this cue's advance-after and the next cue's Time, the column says which one wins, the shorter (Spec 3 §5): "Cue 2's advance-after (8 s) and this cue's Time (12 s) both arm this cue; 8 s wins." Use Previous Video's Length fills Time with the duration of the previous cue's first media write in channel order, within its in and out points, as a number copied once (UX spec).

**The Commands column:**

- **Commands:** the cue's commands other than its channel writes and attribute writes, in their order (stacks, outputs, macros, timers, popups, the system and any other), each as its plain name (Spec 10 §15) with a ⋯ menu: Edit, Move Up, Move Down, Remove (`edit.set`, `edit.move` by position, `edit.remove` on `commands`, Spec 5 §4 as §12 corrects it). An edit held by the cue shows a warning that it changes the library when fired (Spec 5 §2). A command kept verbatim because it is unknown shows Spec 1 §6's warning and can only be removed. The list scrolls within the column.
- **The palette** (E4; a command browser on Adam's word, 2026-11-25): Add Command opens a search field over a tree of the commands, grouped by what they touch and offering only what exists: Playback (`next`, `previous`, `cue N`, a list's or an item's cue, an item's section, `playlist load`, `disarm`), Stacks and outputs (`output N stack M`, `stack N blackout`, `stack N mute CHANNEL`, each with on, off or toggle), Audio (`audio N mute`), Macros (`macro N`; one that would call the macro being edited again is marked Recursive and not offered), Timers (`timer N set DURATION [up]`, `start`, `pause`, `reset`, for the machine's timer slots), Popups (`popup N set "TEXT" [clearafter S]`, `popup N clear`) and System (`system clearall`, `preflight`, `snapshot`, `undo`, `redo`). Picking one shows its small form with pickers beside the tree and its canonical text under the form as it is filled; a popup's text has the Variables ▸ menu; a line at the browser's foot takes any command typed in canonical text (Spec 2 §4). Add inserts the command after the selected one, or at the end. Channels are the grid's rows, which the palette's Channels group focuses. There is no external group in v1 (E4), and the palette offers no edits.
- **Commands as text** (the UX spec's power toggle), a switch at the column's foot, swaps the Commands and Channel columns for one text area: the cue's whole command list, one command per line, in order, in canonical text, parsed as it is typed with OCore's parser (Spec 2 §4), each error beside its line. A multi-line body shows with Spec 2 §2's escapes. A command kept verbatim because it is unknown has no text form, so it shows as its JSON on one locked line and goes back as the object it was (Spec 1 §6). ⌘Return, or leaving the area, sends one `edit.set` of the cue's `commands` with the parsed list, but only while no line has an error: with one, nothing is sent, and leaving asks Keep Editing or Discard Changes, Discard never the default. Escape discards.

**The Channel column** is headed by the selected row's channel, "Channel 2 · Foreground", and with the header row selected by the channel last selected. It holds the three-way control, the content and the attribute chips for the selected cell.

- **Write, Clear, Hold** (the UX spec's three states, Hold the default). Each sends one `edit.cueChannel` for that cue and channel (Spec 5 §4), keeping the channel's standalone attribute writes. Clear writes `empty`; Hold removes the content command. Leaving a write for Clear or Hold keeps each of its `track` riders as a standalone attribute write and drops its `once` riders, so what tracks from this cue on doesn't change (Spec 3 §3). Write on a held or cleared cell opens the content popover (below), and the cell writes nothing until something is chosen; a new pick for a write keeps its transition and audio-transition riders and the attribute writes, as a drop does (Spec 10 §10). On a "No channel" row the only action is Hold, sent as `edit.remove` of those commands in one group.
- **Content.** For a write, a small tile and the media's name or the first line of the text; for a hold, the same for what the channel holds, with the cue it came from ("From cue 3"); a dash for a clear or for holding nothing. A click on it, or Return on a selected cell in the grid (§2), opens the content popover, which hangs from that cell in the grid, scrolled into view, so what is edited sits beside its cue (Adam, 2026-11-24).
- **The content popover.** For text, the body in a multi-line field with the cursor at its end (Return commits and closes the popover, ⇧Return makes a line, Escape discards; Adam, 2026-11-24) and a field for each other field the write has, with Add Field for `title`, `reference`, `copyright` or a custom name, and a Variables ▸ menu that inserts Spec 6 §5's names, which text content resolves; it sends on commit, as Spec 10 §10's in-place text does. For media, the pool's search and tiles to pick from, and for the chosen item its trim (in and out) and loop as overrides on the write (choice A-6). Loop only turns looping on, since a write can't refuse an item's own loop (Spec 3 §4). Its gain and sync offset fall back to the item's defaults, the sync-offset UI being v1.5 (E9). On a held or cleared cell the popover opens on the kind the channel's `usualContent` names, with a switch to the other. A double-click on a cell opens the popover too; on a text write it opens the Text page on this cue's chunk instead (UX spec), once Area 10 lands.
- **Attribute chips:** transition, audio transition (E9), grade and gain, each showing its value and its scope, "this cue only" (`once`) or "from here on" (`track`), or a faint + and its name for one the cue doesn't write. A click opens a popover with the value (None or a grade; a transition whose type applies to pixels; an audio transition; decibels for gain), a duration where the command takes one, the scope, and Remove. On a write the chip is a rider; with Clear or Hold it is a standalone attribute command, which is always `track` (Spec 1 §6), so its popover offers no scope. Each change is one `edit.cueChannel`. Chase and build are v2.
- **Tracking off** (Spec 3 §3 rule 7). A new chip for that attribute is "this cue only". A standalone command reads "this cue only", since that is how it plays here, and an existing `track` rider keeps its scope when its value changes, so a master edited at a campus with tracking off still tracks at the campuses that track.

**Multi-cue editing** (E5). With several cues selected, the Preview monitor shows the first, and the inspector is reduced (UX spec): the Cue column keeps only the section, the Trigger column stays, the Commands column says to select one cue, and the Channel column keeps the selected row's chips, without the three-way control or the content. A field whose cues differ reads "Mixed". A change sends one group with one edit per selected cue: `edit.set` for the section and the trigger, and for a chip `edit.cueChannel`, keeping each cue's own content state on that channel. "This cue only" applies to the selected cues that write content on that channel and skips the rest, saying how many it skipped, since on a cue that clears or holds the channel an attribute command always tracks. The pair warning counts every selected cue.

## 5. The text editor

The Text page shows one list's text on one channel as a single scrolling document, cue breaks as rules between chunks (UX spec). It is Area 10.

**Which text** (E1). The header's channel picker lists the channels, those whose `usualContent` is text first. The page opens on the lowest-numbered text channel (channel 2 on first run), or on the channel whose cell was double-clicked in the Cues page. It edits the `body` field of that channel's text writes; other fields, other channels and other commands are left as they are, unless a chunk is emptied (R5, below).

**The document.** One chunk per cue, in the list's order, every cue a chunk, even one that writes nothing on this channel (E2). Between chunks, a break: a rule carrying the next cue's number. In the left margin, the section's label at the first chunk of each run of cues that share it, in the section's colour (Spec 10 §6). Beside each chunk, the cue's thumbnail on the thumbnail stack, the engine's file, which updates as the engine redraws it (the `thumbnails` topic), so the look of a typed line shows within a moment. The text itself is plain, in the system font: text has no look of its own here (UX spec). Spelling is underlined but never corrected, and text replacement is off, so "Thee" stays "Thee". Text still being composed (an accent, dictation) isn't sent until it is entered.

**A chunk shows** what the cue does on the channel, from the app's own fold of the list from its top (Spec 10 §6):

- **A text write:** its body, editable.
- **A clear:** an empty chunk, with "Clears" as its placeholder (R5).
- **Hold:** the line the channel holds at that cue, greyed, or "Holds nothing". Typing into a held text turns it into a write: a copy of the held write with the typing in its body (R5). Over held media ("Holds Video: Ocean loop") or nothing, the write is only what was typed.
- **Other content** written on this channel (media, a slide, an input): a row naming it ("Video: Ocean loop"), not editable. A designed slide's tagged text belongs to the slide designer (v2).

**Selection** stays within one chunk, so no keystroke ever reaches two cues. A second ⌘A selects the whole document, for Copy only. Typing, Delete, Cut or Paste over a selection that crosses a break does nothing and says "Select within one cue." Text can't be dragged, and Find has no Replace in v1.

**Typing** sends one `edit.cueChannel` for that cue and channel: the chunk's whole body, with the rest of what the cue says about the channel (its other fields, its riders, its attribute writes) as the latest `library` event left it. It sends at most four times a second while typing, and once when the chunk loses the caret; the engine folds a second of it into one edit and one undo step (Spec 5 §5). A chunk left empty is a clear (R5): the editor sends the clear when the chunk loses the caret empty, keeping the write's `track` riders as standalone attribute writes as the Cues page does (§4), and never sends an empty text write. A chunk the caret passes through without typing sends nothing.

**Near the live cue** (choice A-14). The chunks of the three cues after the live cue, in playlist order, can show at once in a box at +1 to +3, such as the confidence screen's (§2); near a song's end they are the next song's first cues (Spec 3 §7). They send when the caret leaves them, not while typing, and say "On the confidence screen", so half-typed words never reach the stage.

**Breaks** (E2). A chunk is bound to its cue by the cue's uuid, not by its place, so typing in a chunk changes only that cue's text write and never moves another cue's commands.

- **Split:** ⌥Return, or Edit ▸ Split Cue, at the caret. The text above stays with the cue; a new cue right after it takes the text below, in a write with the other fields of the one it was split from and none of its riders, with every other channel held and the label and section copied. One group: `edit.cueChannel`, then `edit.insert` with a uuid the app makes (Spec 5 §5, §4). At the end of a chunk the new cue's chunk is empty, which is a clear until something is typed into it.
- **Merge:** Backspace at the start of a chunk joins it to the chunk above, and forward Delete at the end of a chunk joins the one below to it. The first cue keeps its commands and the joined text, and the second cue is removed. A merge takes a fresh press: a held key stops at the chunk's edge. The editor asks first when the second cue has anything beyond its text on this channel (other commands, riders or attribute writes on this channel, a trigger other than Go, an advance-after or a note), or is the live or the armed cue: "Cue 5 also writes channel 1 and runs macro 3. Merging removes them." Merge is never the sheet's default. One group, `edit.cueChannel` then `edit.remove`.
- **Moving a break:** a break drags to another line boundary, moving those lines from one chunk to its neighbour: one group of two `edit.cueChannel` edits. A break never passes a chunk's last line.
- **What joins.** A split, a merge or a moved break works between chunks of text, clears and holds, a clear or a hold counting as empty text, which becomes a write when text moves into it, with the other fields of the write the text came from. None reaches into a row of other content: Backspace at the start of a chunk under such a row does nothing.

**Paste** (E2, E3, choice A-4) takes plain text; styled text pastes as its plain text, line by line.

- **Breaks.** A line that is empty or only spaces is blank, and a run of blank lines is one break.
- **Section lines.** A line that is only a section name isn't text: it is a break, as a blank line is, and sets the section of the chunk after it (E3). The names are E3's, case-insensitive, with an optional number (Intro, Verse, Verse 1, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Tag, Interlude, Instrumental, Outro, Ending), and may end in a colon or sit in brackets or parentheses ("Verse 1:", "\[Chorus\]"). A section line with no text before the next section line or the end, such as a bare "Instrumental", makes one empty cue in that section, which clears.
- **Repeats.** A repeat mark on its own line, or at the end of a section line ("(2x)", "2x", "x2"), repeats that section's cues so it runs that many times in a row, each copy with new uuids, since a list is played in order.
- **SongSelect's footer.** A closing block that starts with a line beginning "CCLI Song #" isn't lyrics: its song number fills the list's CCLI number and its © line its copyright, where they are empty, and the rest is dropped.
- **Into a list with no cues:** one cue per chunk, all in one group (Spec 5 §5), each holding every other channel, with its section as its label and its section. Pasting a lyric sheet into a new list makes the song in one step, and one undo takes it back.
- **Into a chunk** of a list that has cues: the paste goes in at the caret as if typed with a split at each break. The chunk keeps the text before the caret and the first paragraph; each later paragraph becomes a new cue after it, in a write with the other fields of the chunk's, the last one also taking the text that followed the caret. One group, with the chunk's own edit.
- **No breaks.** A paste of more than four lines with no blank lines and no section lines asks how to break it: Every Line a Cue, Two Lines per Cue or Keep in This Cue. A shorter one is typing.
- **Paste as Two-Line Cues** (⌥⌘V, choice A-15) makes a cue of every two lines within each paragraph, an odd last line alone, with the rest of the rules above, and never asks.

**An empty list** shows one empty chunk saying "Type or paste the song's text". The first character typed creates the first cue and its write as one group, and typing goes on from there.

**The right column:** the list's metadata (title, author, copyright, CCLI number, track through), each an `edit.set` on the list on commit, which field boxes and context values show from the next frame (Spec 5 §2, P12); and, for the chunk with the caret, its cue's label and section (a combo box with the list's sections and E3's names) and Hold, which returns the channel to hold on that cue, keeping `track` riders as §4 says.

**Undo** (choice A-5). ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z are Edit ▸ Undo and Redo, the library's (Spec 5 §8), while the last change is this editor's own: its open second of typing, or the history's top step when it is a group this editor sent, which it knows from the results' `group` (Spec 10 §3). A step is a second of typing, a split, a merge, a moved break or a paste. ⌘Z sends the chunk's unsent text, then `undo`, which closes the run and takes it back (Spec 5 §5). Otherwise ⌘Z in the editor does nothing and names the last change ("The last change was to stack 1"), so it never undoes what another page, station or source did; Edit ▸ Undo, naming it, still undoes it. An undo or a redo replaces every chunk it names, the caret's included, keeping the caret's place in its text where it can. The text view keeps no undo of its own, so its text never parts from the library's.

**Keys.** The editor is a text field (Spec 10 §9): it takes every key but ⇧⎋, a binding that includes `ctrl`, and the function keys. Return makes a new line within the chunk; ⌥Return splits, as a slide break is made in the reflow editor operators know; ⌘Return leaves the editor, as it commits elsewhere; Tab and ⇧Tab move the focus on (Spec 10 §15). Escape leaves the editor and keeps what was typed, which has already been sent; ⌘Z takes it back. While the editor has the focus the top bar says "Editing text: ⌥Return splits · Esc leaves" (Spec 10 §8).

**Other clients.** A `library` event patches the chunks it names, never moving the caret or the scroll. The chunk with the caret keeps its text, takes the rest of its cue from the event, and says "Changed elsewhere" until it next sends. If its cue is removed elsewhere, its text stays, marked "Cue removed", with Add as New Cue and Copy, and nothing is sent until one is chosen. A local copy, or a list on a `readOnlyMasters` machine, opens read-only (§1).

**What is live.** Editing the live list is allowed: each cue shows its edit at its next fire (L3), and the three after the live cue at once on the confidence screen, hence the rule above.

## 6. The stack editor

The Stacks page edits this machine's stacks, and each output's default stack and transitions (UX spec, E6). Stacks and outputs are machine-level, so the page stays editable on a `readOnlyMasters` machine (Spec 5 §7), apart from its audio routing table, which edits channels.

**The list** (§2) is the machine's stacks, the live dot on those an output shows. Its + row is New Stack, and a row's ⋯ menu has Duplicate, Renumber… and Delete (below).

**The center** is two tall columns (Adam, 2026-11-24). The selected stack's live picture, its preview surface before blackout (Spec 10 §2), is the Preview monitor's, its picker naming the stack. The first column holds its slots as a vertical list, the bottom slot at the bottom (UX spec). Each row has the channel's number and label, its preset and its opacity:

- **Preset:** a picker offering None, which means the stack's default, else the channel's, else Preset 1 (Spec 6 §2), or a preset by number and label (`edit stack S slot C preset P`). The row names the preset the slot resolves to, and its menu's Edit Preset opens the preset editor on it, at this stack's tab (§8).
- **Opacity:** a slider from 0 to 100%, with a field beside it that takes a typed percentage (`… opacity 0.8`).
- **No mute column** (choice A-12). Muting a slot is the strip's stack dots, at runtime (Spec 10 §7); the slot's stored `mute` stays as it was written.

Rows drag to a new place (`edit stack S slot C move K`), and Move Up and Move Down in a row's menu do the same by keyboard. Under the rows, the list's + row, Add Channel, offers the library's channels the stack doesn't hold, since a channel appears once per stack (P13); one chosen adds a slot at the stack's top (`edit stack S insert slots K {"channel": C, "preset": null, "opacity": 1, "mute": false}`). A row's menu has Remove from Stack (`edit stack S slot C remove`). On a stack an output shows, adding or removing a slot asks first, since it changes the picture at once (Spec 5 §2): "Channel 3's content and its static boxes will show on output 1 now" or "Channel 2 is on air on output 1; removing its slot takes it off."

**The stack's own fields,** under the slots: label; base, black or transparent; default preset, None or a preset; and gamma blend, with "for matching an older look" (Spec 4 §9). Each is an `edit.set`. The list's + row and a row's menu: New Stack (`edit stack create`, with an empty label the page shows as "Stack N" until one is typed); Duplicate (`edit stack S duplicate`); Renumber…, with swap for a taken number and Spec 5 §4's warning about the portable references that then name another stack, refused while an output shows the stack, and Swap while an output shows either stack (`onAir`, a Spec 5 §6 addition, §12); and Delete, refused while an output shows the stack (E-7) and asking first when anything refers to it, with the counts from `uses` (§3).

**The second column:**

- **Outputs:** each output with its number and label, its live stack, its default stack and its state from the `outputs` topic (Spec 10 §3); then, under "Not on an output", the stacks no output shows (UX spec). A click on an output selects the stack it shows; a click on a stack selects that stack.
- **The selected output's properties.** Its device, format, colour range and genlock, read-only with Change in Settings…, which opens Settings ▸ Outputs on it (E6, Spec 10 §11). Editable here: its default stack (`edit output N defaultStack M`), and its switch and blackout transitions (`edit output N transitions.switch T`, `… transitions.blackout T`), offering transitions that apply to pixels. Set as Default makes the output's live stack its default stack (UX spec).
- **Audio routing:** a table of channels by audio outputs, each cell a checkbox that sends `edit channel N audioOutputs [...]` with the channel's new list (UX spec), keeping the numbers this machine has no audio output for, since another campus meets them (Spec 1 §2). It is the same field the channel sheet edits (§7).

## 7. The channel sheet

The Channels page edits the library's channels (UX spec). Channels are portable, so a channel edit reaches every campus the library syncs to.

**The list** (§2) is the library's channels, each with its number, label, usual content and the stacks that hold it (UX spec). Its + row is New Channel, and a row's ⋯ menu has Duplicate and Delete (below).

**The center** is the selected channel's form, one tall column (UX spec; Adam, 2026-11-24), and beside it the stacks that hold it (below). Its live picture, the strip's (Spec 10 §7), is the Preview monitor's, its picker naming the channel:

- **Number.** Changing it sends `edit channel N renumber M`, which carries every reference in the library and this machine's root with it (Spec 1 §4, Spec 5 §4); a taken number offers Swap (`swap`). Before it sends, a popover says what doesn't follow: "OSC, console and Stream Deck maps that name channel 2 aren't changed, and other campuses' stacks and key bindings still name channel 2 until they're changed there" (UX spec). Renumber is refused while the channel has content in a stack an output shows, and Swap while either channel has content and either is in a stack an output shows (`onAir`, as for Delete), since content on air is held by its channel's number (Spec 3 §2). A channel whose content is sounding on an audio output is on air too, for Renumber, Swap and Delete alike (Spec 5 §6).
- **Label.**
- **Default preset,** a preset by number and label (Spec 6 §2's last fallback before Preset 1).
- **Default transition**, **default audio transition** and **default gain** (decibels, within ±60), which the channel's attributes reset to (Spec 3 §3); an edit shows at the next list boundary, `channel N reset` or clear all (Spec 5 §2).
- **Decoder cap,** 2 or 3, with its cost: one more decoder session and its frame pool, about 100 to 200 MB at 4K (Spec 4 §5, §6).
- **Audio outputs:** a checkbox per audio output on this machine (`audioOutputs`), keeping the numbers this machine has none for (§6), the same field as the stack editor's table.
- **Text thumbnail,** body or first line: how the strip and the pickers show its text (Spec 1 §3, Spec 10 §7).
- **Usual content,** media, text, slide, live or any: the hint that picks the text channel for in-place editing and the text editor (Spec 10 §6, §5 here) and orders pickers.
- **Render size,** largest, or a width and height (Spec 1 §2).

Each field is an `edit.set` on commit, apart from the number.

**Beside the form,** a second column lists the stacks that hold the channel (UX spec), each with Remove from Stack, and Add to Stack ▸ for the others, which adds a slot at the stack's top, asking first on a stack an output shows (§6).

**The list's + row and a row's menu:** New Channel (`edit channel create`, with the first-run channels' defaults, Spec 6 §9, and an empty label the page shows as "Channel N" until one is typed); Duplicate (`edit channel N duplicate`); Delete. A channel with content on an output's stack, or sounding on an audio output, can't be deleted (E-7, `onAir`). Otherwise Delete asks first, with the counts from `uses`: "12 cues in 3 lists write channel 5. They stay in the lists, and the reference report flags them until they're edited" (E7).

## 8. The preset editor

The Presets page edits a preset's layouts and their boxes, and the text styles boxes carry (UX spec, E8, C4). Presets are portable: an edit reaches every campus that shares the library.

**The list** (§2) is the presets, and under them the boxes of the layout shown (below). The presets' + row is New Preset, and a row's ⋯ menu has Duplicate and Delete (below). The center is the layout tabs, the canvas and the sample; the Preview monitor shows the live picture of the stack the canvas is drawn at, its picker naming that stack; and the inspector's columns hold the selected box's properties, its text style, and the preset with Used by.

**Layouts** (E8). Across the top of the center, a tab for the `*` layout and one per stack this machine has, each naming the stacks it draws on this machine ("Any stack: 1 Main, 3 Side"; "2 Confidence"), with the live dot on those an output shows. A stack's tab without a layout of its own says "Uses the \* layout" and offers Copy from \*, which sets `layouts.S` to a copy of the `*` layout (`edit.set`). A stack's own layout offers Remove This Stack's Layout (`edit preset P unset layouts.S`), asking first. A layout for a stack this machine doesn't have stays in the preset, listed under "Other stacks", since it meets that number at other campuses (Spec 1 §2). The first edit in a run to a layout on air names its stacks and outputs, "and every campus that shares this preset" (§2).

**The canvas** is the layout at its stack's size (§3), the `*` layout at the size of the stack the Preview monitor's picker names (the thumbnail stack at first). Its picture is the engine's (choice A-1): `render` with `subject.kind` `layout` and the sample (§3), asked for again after each edit. Over it the app draws each box's outline and handles, labelled with its binding ("body", "pixels", "static") and its offset when it isn't 0 ("+1").

- **Selecting.** A click selects a box, and so does a row of the box list, which is how boxes are chosen by keyboard; Tab and ⇧Tab leave the canvas (Spec 10 §15).
- **Moving.** Dragging moves the box and the handles resize it. With the canvas focused, the arrows nudge the selected box by one pixel of the stack, and with ⇧ by ten (§2); a held nudge sends one edit, when the key is released. Boxes snap to the canvas's edges and centre lines and to other boxes' edges, not while ⌘ is held. The outline follows the pointer; when the drag or the nudge ends, one `edit.set` of the box's `frame`, normalized to the stack's size (Spec 1 §2), and the picture follows.
- **Overflow and fonts.** A text box whose sample text overflows has a red outline and says how many lines were cut, from the render's `overflow` (Spec 6 §4). A box whose font fell back says so, from `fallbackFonts`: "DM Sans Bold isn't installed or bundled; drawn in the fallback font."

**The sample** (UX spec: real text and a real frame) is chosen under the canvas, and is the app's alone, never saved in the preset. Kind: text, media or empty.

- **Text:** the fields of the cue selected in the Cues page when it writes text, with that cue as the sample's `listUuid` and `cue` (§3); else a built-in sample, the first verse of "Great Is Thy Faithfulness" (1923, public domain), with a title and a copyright line. Either can be changed in the sample's own field. For boxes at an offset, the next cues' text, or the built-in sample's next verses.
- **Media:** the engine's test pattern at first (§3), or a pool item chosen after it, as its poster or still (§3).

**The preset and Used by,** the inspector's last column:

- **The preset:** its number (renumber, or swap for a taken number) and label; New Preset (`edit preset create`, with an empty `*` layout), Duplicate (`edit preset P duplicate`, the UX spec's duplicate-from, opening the copy) and Delete, which asks first, with the counts from `uses`. Preset 1 can be edited but not deleted, renumbered or swapped (Spec 5 §6). The campus-override view is v1.5 (§1).
- **Used by,** from `uses` (§3): the channels that default to it, the stacks that default to it, the slots that name it, and the slots that reach it by fallback (Spec 6 §2). The engine's warning about a stack default preset with no pixel box or no body box at offset 0 (Spec 5 §6, Spec 6 §3) shows here.
**Boxes,** under the presets in the list column, in z-order with the top box first (the layout's `boxes` run bottom to top), each with its binding and its offset. Rows drag to reorder (`edit.move` on `layouts.L.boxes`, by position, a Spec 5 §4 addition, §12), and Move Up and Move Down in a row's menu do the same by keyboard. The boxes' + row, Add Box, offers Pixels, Field ▸ (body, title, copyright, reference, or a custom name) and Static Text, and inserts a box at the top with a frame in the canvas's middle (`edit.insert`); a row's menu also has Duplicate, which inserts a copy above it, and Remove (`edit.remove`), which Delete does with the canvas or the list focused.

**The selected box's properties** (Spec 1 §2), each an `edit.set` of `layouts.L.boxes.I.FIELD` on commit:

- **Binding:** pixels, a field by name, or static text. Static text is a field with a Variables ▸ menu that inserts Spec 6 §5's names (`[Time]`, `[Date]`, `[Timer N]`, `[Popup N]`, `[Service In]` and the context values), sent on commit. Changing between pixels and text sets the whole box in one edit, so it never holds half of each: a box that becomes text gets `shrinkToFit` and text style 1's value (the type's defaults when there is no style 1), and one that becomes pixels gets `fit` and no style.
- **Frame:** x, y, width and height as percentages of the stack, the same values the canvas drags, read from a reference point picked from nine (Adam, 2026-11-24): a corner, the middle of an edge, or the centre. X and Y say where that point of the box sits, and a new width or height keeps it in place. The box keeps its `frame` as left, top, width and height (Spec 1 §2); the point is the app's, kept in `view.json`.
- **Fit:** for pixels, fill, fit, stretch or crop; for text, shrink to fit with its minimum size, or at most N lines at the style's size (Spec 6 §4).
- **Transition:** Channel's, which a new box takes, or cut or dissolve, the box transitions v1 draws, as the box's own, with a duration of its own or the transition's (Spec 1 §2: it overrides the channel's default transition, never one a cue sets); and transition on variable change, which a popup box wants (UX spec). A pixel box takes the channel's and shows no choice (Adam, 2026-11-25).
- **Fallback:** draws only when no earlier box at the same offset drew (Spec 6 §3).
- **Offset:** 0 to +3; a box at an offset shows the channel as it will be after that many cues, a pixel box as a poster (Spec 6 §3). The schema's −2 and −1 show as v1.5.
- **Grade:** None or a grade, the box's own correction, apart from the channel's.

**The text style** of a text box (C4) is the box's inline style (Spec 1 §2), shown as a form:

- font, from the `fonts` query's families and faces, the engine's installed and bundled fonts (Spec 6 §4); size, with its unit, points on the 1080-line canvas or a percentage of the output's height; weight, or the axes of a variable font; colour; alignment and vertical alignment; line spacing; letter spacing; outline, its width and colour; shadow, its x, y, blur and colour; all caps. Each change sets one field of `textStyle`.
- **From a numbered style.** The form says which style the box came from (`fromStyle`), and "changed" once its values differ. Apply Style ▸ copies a numbered style's value into the box and sets `fromStyle` (one `edit.set` of `textStyle`). Re-apply copies the style's current value again. Save as Style… asks for a name and sends one group: `edit textstyle N create` with the box's style, then the box's `fromStyle` set to N. N is one more than the highest style number in the catalog or named by any box's `fromStyle`, the number the engine itself would pick (Spec 5 §4), so a new style never adopts a deleted one's boxes; a number taken meanwhile fails the group, and the app tries the next. A style is copied when applied (Spec 1 §2), so changing the box never changes the style.
- **Fonts in the app.** The app registers the bundled fonts for its own process too, from the engine's resources in the same bundle, so the font picker and the style form draw each face in itself. What goes on air is still the engine's drawing.

## 9. The small editors

The Objects page is the UX spec's one parameter-editor frame (C4), laid out by §2's rule as Adam settled it on 2026-11-25. The page's list holds the objects in groups (Grades, Transitions, Audio transitions, Text styles, Macros), each with its objects by number and label and a + row that creates one; a row's ⋯ menu, and its right-click menu, have Duplicate (the UX spec's duplicate-from), Renumber… (or swap for a taken number, Spec 5 §4) and Delete. The workspace holds the selected object in one column, its number and label at the column's top and **Used by** (from `uses`, §3) at its foot, and gives the rest of its width to the object's preview, the stage. Delete asks first, with the counts from `uses`: the references stay, and the reference report flags them (Spec 5 §4, E7). Transitions 1–3 have no Delete and can't be renumbered or swapped (Spec 5 §6); a copy of one is a new transition. The Preview monitor shows the live picture of the stack its picker names, as on the Stacks page.

**Grades** (C4). Each parameter is a slider with a number field and its identity marked (Spec 1 §2): brightness, contrast and saturation from 0 to 200% (100% is identity), hue from −180° to 180° (0°), opacity from 0 to 100% (100%), blur from 0 to the largest radius the grade pass takes (0), and tint, a colour well (white is identity), as Spec 4 §7 applies them. Each sends one `edit.set` when its drag ends. The stage is the engine's picture of a pool item through the grade beside the same item without it (`render` with `subject.kind` `grade`, and `grade` null for the second, §3), the item picked over them, at first the pool's first video or image. A grade on air changes from the next frame (Spec 5 §2).

**Transitions** (C4; new ones on Adam's word, 2026-11-25, G-38): objects of the three types v1 draws (cut, dissolve and fade through a color); new types, such as wipes, are still v2's custom transitions. New (a 500 ms dissolve), Duplicate, Renumber… and Delete as for grades, but for transitions 1–3, which ship built in and stay protected (Spec 5 §6). Every transition's duration (`ms`), easing and, for a fade, color can be edited; the type of a new one is picked from the three, and 1–3 keep theirs (`protected`). The stage plays the transition from one pool item to another, both picked over it (at first the pool's first two videos or images, or black for a missing one), with Play, a loop switch and a scrub bar over the easing curve drawn across the duration. The two pictures are the engine's (`render` with `subject.kind` `grade` and `grade` null); the app mixes them as Spec 4 §7 says the channel pass does (a dissolve's mix by the eased progress, fade through a colour to the colour by the first half and from it by the second, a cut at the trigger), so the stage shows the timing, not the engine's own drawing of the mix. A transition already running keeps the timing it started with (Spec 5 §2).

**Audio transitions** (E9, choice A-7): type (cut, crossfade, fade through silence), duration, easing, and the offset, signed and measured from the trigger, that makes J and L cuts (UX spec). New, Duplicate and Delete as for grades. The stage draws the outgoing and incoming gains over time on two lanes, with the trigger and the picture's cut marked, as Spec 4 §11 applies them, and Play runs a playhead across them. The preview is silent: the app has no audio output of its own in a show, and the Mac's default output may be the room.

**Text styles** (choice A-8): the numbered styles, edited with the style form of §8 (`edit textstyle N FIELD VALUE`). The stage draws a sample in the style on the shape of the thumbnail stack, the built-in sample text (§8) at first and a field over the stage to change it; it is the app's drawing with the same fonts (§8, "Fonts in the app"), an approximation of the engine's raster. Used by lists the boxes that name the style in `fromStyle`. Re-apply to Boxes copies the style's value into every one of them, as one group of `edit.set`s of each box's `textStyle`, after saying how many. Deleting a style leaves each box its own copy; their `fromStyle` then names nothing, which the reference report flags.

**Macros** (UX spec): the column holds the tile's colour and label (for the Macros tab, Spec 10 §7), the scope as a tag, computed (Machine for a macro that names a stack or an output, which the engine moves to the machine root, Spec 1 §2, Spec 5 §2; else Library), and Run on Air; the stage holds the commands.

- **Commands,** in order, each a row with its plain name (Spec 10 §15) and Edit, Move Up, Move Down and Remove, added with the palette's browser (§4). A macro has no channel rows, so here the browser's Channels group adds channel commands: a write from the pool, a text write, clear, reset, play, pause, stop, end behaviour, lock, freeze, and the grade, transition, audio transition and gain commands. Commands as Text works as in §4. A row says what is wrong with it in a short tag, not a sentence: Recursive (it calls a macro that calls this one, the engine's `cycle`, Spec 5 §6), Missing (it names a macro, a channel, a stack, an output, a grade or a transition that doesn't exist), Unknown (kept verbatim, Spec 1 §6), and Edits library (it holds an edit). A refusal of an edit of the macro is said the same way.
- **Run on Air** (choice A-10) commits an open field first, then sends `macro N` with source `ui`: at once while nothing is live, and after asking while something is. A macro that holds an edit says first "This also changes the library."
- **Used by:** cues, key bindings and scheduler entries, other macros, and the startup and shutdown macros.
- No guards: they are v1.5 (Spec 2 §7).

## 10. Accessibility and localization

Spec 10 §15 holds in edit mode, from the first build. What edit mode adds:

- **VoiceOver.** The cue grid is a table. Its column headers are the cues, each saying its number, label, section, trigger, and "live" on the live cue; its row headers are the channels, each saying its number and label and whether it is folded; and a cell says what the cue does there ("Cue 3, channel 2 Foreground: writes text, That saved a wretch like me, dissolve 0.5 seconds"; "holds Amber Haze from cue 1"; "clears"), with its attribute writes after it. The zoom slider says the column's width, and the monitors say what they show ("Preview: cue 3 on stack 1 Main"; "Program: stack 1 Main, on air on outputs 1 and 2"). The three-way control is a segmented control that names its state ("Channel 2 Foreground: write, text"), and a chip is a button whose label says its value and scope ("Grade 3, from here on"). On a canvas each box is an element that says its binding, offset and frame in percent, with actions to move and resize it by 1% of the stack. In the text editor, the caret entering a chunk announces its cue ("Cue 4, Chorus"), and a split, a merge or a moved break says what it did ("New cue 5 after cue 4"). A rejection and a warning are announced where they show.
- **Keyboard.** Every action has a keyboard path: the pages from the View menu (⌥⌘1 to ⌥⌘6); the cue grid, lists and canvases with the arrows (§2); the zoom with the arrows on its slider; a row's fold with its control; reordering with Move Earlier, Move Later, Move Up and Move Down; a box chosen in the box list and its frame typed in its fields; a selected thing's menu with ⌃Return (Spec 10 §15).
- **Colour is never alone.** A section's colour comes with its label, an overflowing box's red outline with how many lines were cut, a mixed value with "Mixed", a read-only page with its reason.
- **Reduce Motion** drops the canvas's snapping animation and the cue grid's scrolling animation.
- **Localization.** Every string is in the app's catalog (Spec 10 §15). The section names, repeat marks and SongSelect's footer are matched on paste as the catalog lists them, English in v1, so a translated catalog brings its own. Commands in the commands lists read with Spec 10 §15's plain names.
